# Gist — Agnibeena

**Gist:** A young poet strings a lute of fire and, through song, summons the sleeping masses of a colonized land to rise against bondage and claim their own dawn.

**Expansion:** In October 1922, a 23-year-old former soldier named Kazi Nazrul Islam publishes his first collection of verse, *Agnibeena* (The Fiery Lyre), and detonates a literary thunderbolt across colonial Bengal. Against the serene mysticism of Tagore's age, Nazrul brings a soldier's cadence and an activist's rage — twelve poems that fuse Hindu and Islamic imagery into a fierce secular humanism. The collection's crown jewel, *Bidrohi* (The Rebel), proclaims an "I" that is storm, volcano, and dawn, refusing to quiet until the cries of the oppressed cease. The poems challenge empire, censorship, and religious bigotry alike, and their fire is later carried onto the battlefields of the 1971 Liberation War.
