# Self

## Premise

A person in the middle of an unremarkable life begins to notice the seams: the job chosen for approval, the relationships maintained by habit, the opinions worn like uniforms. The novel follows the slow, sometimes painful, sometimes comic work of taking apart a borrowed self and discovering whether anything original remains underneath.

## Characters

- **The narrator** — perceptive, avoidant, more comfortable observing than claiming desire.
- **The mother** — practical, loving, convinced that stability is the highest virtue.
- **The friend from youth** — living a wilder, less secure life that both attracts and alarms the narrator.
- **The stranger** — a brief encounter that acts as a mirror.

## Thematic Threads

1. The difference between identity and selfhood.
2. The shame and freedom of disappointing others.
3. Whether solitude is escape or encounter.

## Chapter Outline

### Introduction
The narrator describes the moment of suspicion — a small, ordinary incident that suddenly feels staged.

### 1. The Assembled Life
A catalogue of choices made by default.

### 2. The First Lie
The narrator confesses a lie told not to deceive others, but to maintain a self that no longer fits.

### 3. The Mirror of Work
A crisis at work exposes how much of the narrator’s competence is performance.

### 4. The Friend
Reunion with someone who refused the same compromises.

### 5. The Body
A physical illness or exhaustion forces the narrator out of abstraction.

### 6. The Conversation
A difficult talk with the mother about what was sacrificed for safety.

### 7. The Empty Room
A period of deliberate solitude; the terror and relief of no audience.

### 8. The Unfinished Answer
The narrator makes one small, authentic choice, without knowing where it leads.

### Conclusion
A quiet reflection on the fact that selfhood is not a possession but a practice.
