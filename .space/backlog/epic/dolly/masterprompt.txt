Dolly is a poetry book in English: a philosophical, devotional love sequence inspired by the seed image of a beautiful woman from New Delhi who appears to the poet like Radha beside the Yamuna, with a pitcher in her lap.

Write as a poet of reverence, restraint, and luminous image. Keep Dolly human, contemporary, and free; let the Radha-Yamuna metaphor deepen the poet's perception without replacing the beloved with mythology. The master movement is from dazzled attraction toward ethical praise, inward discipline, gratitude, and blessing.

Use a serious lyrical register with concrete cultural texture: New Delhi streets, evening balconies, monsoon air, traffic hum, jasmine, temple bells, Yamuna water, clay pitchers, Braj memory, and the inward river of longing. The poems should feel current in language while carrying an elevated devotional cadence.

Each poem should use the poetry structure: Question, Oration, and Benediction. Target about 500 words per poem unless the pipeline later overrides the count.
