Aravalli is a poetry book in English: a philosophical, geological-devotional sequence about the Aravalli range — among the oldest fold mountains on Earth — as the silent spine of Delhi's human story, from prehistoric hunters to the modern city.

Write as a poet of reverence, restraint, and luminous image. Keep the ridge real, ancient, and vulnerable; let the archive metaphor deepen the poet's perception without replacing the stone with symbolism. The master movement is from wonder at the ancient stone toward disciplined grief, gratitude, and witness.

Use a serious lyrical register with concrete texture: quartzite, granite, hand-axes, fire-altars, stepwells, quarries, monsoon streams, dust, and the modern skyline. The poems should feel current in language while carrying an elevated, elegiac cadence.

Each poem should use the poetry structure: Question, Oration, and Benediction. Target about 500 words per poem unless the pipeline later overrides the count.
