# Behula - Gist

## Gist

Behula, the Bengali heroine of the Manasamangal, sacrifices her social life and braves the river of death to revive her dead husband Lakhindar, her devotion so great that Lord Indra himself honours her character and the bhat flower weeps upon her foot.

## Expansion

Behula is the speaking presence of this book: daughter-in-law, wife, pilgrim, mourner, and defiant lover. When her husband Lakhindar is bitten by a snake on their wedding night and dies, Behula refuses the widow's silence. She builds a raft, places her husband's body upon it, and sails the river for months, enduring storms, hunger, and the mockery of the world, until she reaches the court of the gods. Her central conflict is not against a single serpent but against fate itself — against the decree of Manasa, the goddess of snakes, and against a society that would bury a woman's love with her husband. The transformation is from a sheltered bride to a woman whose devotion becomes a force that moves heaven: Lord Indra, moved by her character, grants her husband's life back, and the bhat flower, the flower of the riverbank, is said to have wept upon her foot in witness to her sacrifice.
