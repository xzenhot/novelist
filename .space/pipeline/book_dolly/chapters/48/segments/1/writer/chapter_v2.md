# The Poet's Jealousy At The Dehlij

## Question

At the Dehlij of New Delhi, where The Poet's Jealousy opens like a small lamp between Dolly, the remembered Yamuna, and the city that will not slow down, what changes in the poet when beauty asks not to be possessed but witnessed?

## Oration

At the Mahipalpur edge of evening, where aircraft lights descend through smog and the road keeps coughing up the day's heat, the poet stands with Dolly's name in his mouth as if it were a clay cup full of water. New Delhi does not pause for him. Gurgaon keeps its glass lit late into the night; the metro doors open and close with a metallic patience; the Ring Road pulls dust around the city like a tired shawl. Yet beneath this speed, beneath the flyover and the advertisement and the sealed office window, something older turns in the dark. Tethys withdraws. Gondwana drifts. The Aravalli keeps its ancient stone spine under all these hurried careers. The poet hears that old pressure and understands that The Poet's Jealousy is not a decorative moment. It is a coordinate.

Dolly appears in this coordinate not as a prize, not as a figure painted for the poet's hunger, but as a living threshold. She stands between New Delhi and the remembered Yamuna the way a person may stand between two rooms without belonging wholly to either. Behind her, Radha remains a lamp of devotion; before her, the modern city throws dust, noise, bargaining, heat, and impatient light. The poet must not confuse the lamp with the face. He may borrow myth only if he returns the human being freer than he found her. So he lowers his voice. He lets the metaphor kneel. The pitcher in her lap is no longer an ornament for praise; it is a discipline, a hollow shaped by pressure, wheel, hand, fire, and restraint.

The theme moving beneath this styled chapter is The Uncluttered Soul, the lesson of a gaze washed clean of appetite. In the Dehlij voice it arrives through a gaze washed of hunger, like a courtyard after first rain. The poet carries this lesson inside the body because the body is the first temple and the last map. His ribs become the Ridge, half-stone and half-breath. His veins remember the Yamuna, sacred and wounded at once. His throat becomes the Sahibi, dry for long months, then suddenly flooded by one honest line. He understands that longing is not proved by loudness. Longing is proved by the care with which one refuses to harm what one loves.

His father's teaching does not enter as sermon. It enters as ache. Jnan Gosai had known that God could be found in the body; here, in Delhi NCR, the son has to search for that same hidden god in a body-city of dust, petrol, office fatigue, market light, old tombs, new towers, and exhausted breath. Kalkaji's drums answer the stone of the Ridge. Chandni Chowk's crowded lanes answer the lost canals of Bengal. A memory of Bahirgachi rises without announcement: khal water, bil mist, wet earth after rain, a quiet house where language did not need to prove itself. Then the present returns, hard and fluorescent. The poet does not choose one room over the other. He stands on the lintel. This standing is Dehlij.

Therefore Dolly's beauty must be approached as one approaches a sacred river in a damaged city: with reverence, with shame, with the knowledge that worship can also pollute when it thinks only of itself. He must not pour adjectives into the Yamuna and call the foam devotion. He must not write Dolly into a chamber where she did not choose to stand. He must let her errands, refusals, laughter, silences, and inward weather remain hers. Praise becomes ethical only when it keeps the beloved unowned. A name becomes prayer only when the hand that writes it opens again.

The poet lists what he sees, because listing is how he survives the giant city. Metro window. Tea steam. Dust on the balcony rail. A temple bell swallowed by traffic. A torn poster near a drain. A patch of dark water under a bridge. Dolly's name beside Yamuna, Radha, pitcher, Delhi, distance, dignity. To name is not to possess; to name is to keep from forgetting. This is why the list must remain humble. The city has forgotten too many layers already. It walks over forts, lost water, old prayers, partitioned rooms, and buried alphabets without lowering its voice. The poet lowers his voice for all of them.

So The Poet's Jealousy At The Dehlij becomes not a monument but a walk. The poem moves through New Delhi with bare feet inside its own language, careful of heat, careful of broken stone, careful of the beloved's freedom. It lets Radha remain luminous without turning Dolly into a symbol. It lets the pitcher hold water without making the water a cage. It lets the Yamuna flow inside conscience, even when the visible river is tired, fenced, and dark. The poet learns that beauty is not an answer given to desire; beauty is the question desire must become worthy to ask.

When this styled version ends, it does not close the door. It leaves the poet standing where Delhi and Bengal meet without merging, where the dry Sahibi waits under months of dust, where the body remembers what the city forgets. From there the next poem, The Garland Refused, can approach with a cleaner breath. Let Dolly remain beyond the page, alive in her own day. Let the poet keep only the work of attention. Let the threshold hold him a little longer, one foot in dust, one foot in remembered water, listening for the river under concrete.

## Benediction

May Dolly remain free beyond every metaphor; may the poet stand humbly at the Dehlij of his own longing; and may the reader move toward The Garland Refused with a gaze washed by dust, river, memory, and restraint.
