# The Hand That Holds Water

## Question

What changes in the poet when The Hand That Holds Water reveals Dolly not as an object of possession, but as a living threshold between New Delhi and the remembered Yamuna?

## Oration

At the hand-pump the woman cups her palms,
receives the water, drinks, and lets
the rest fall back to the mud —
nothing imprisoned, nothing owned.

A gesture older than scripture:
the hand that holds without hoarding,
the palm a roof, not a fist.

I have hoarded, poet that I am —
kept syllables in locked boxes,
reread my inventory of her
till the keeping spoiled the kept.

Teach me, water, your open arithmetic:
what is received with welcome
returns to the world with gratitude,
and only the cupped hand — never the cage —
deserves to be called holy.

## Benediction

Let the closing blessing leave Dolly free, the poet humbled, and the reader carried toward 'Delhi Heat, Yamuna Memory' with a quieter, cleaner gaze.
