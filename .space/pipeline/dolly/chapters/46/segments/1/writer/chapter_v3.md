# The Weight Of The Pitcher

## Question

What changes in the poet when The Weight Of The Pitcher reveals Dolly not as an object of possession, but as a living threshold between New Delhi and the remembered Yamuna?

## Oration

See the woman with the water pot:
arm angled, spine certain,
beauty that includes labor —
the balance learned from a thousand trips,
the strength no hymn bothers to mention.

We praise faces and forget the carrying.
We worship grace and cheat the muscle.

But the pitcher does not lift itself.
Someone lifts it, every day,
and the lifting is part of the loveliness.

So honor the embodied one, poet:
her walk is an engineering of patience,
her poise a daily negotiation with gravity.

Dolly is not an image resting on paper.
She is weight, balance, breath, and stride —
a living person carrying water
through the exact middle of the world.

## Benediction

Let the closing blessing leave Dolly free, the poet humbled, and the reader carried toward 'A River Under Asphalt' with a quieter, cleaner gaze.
