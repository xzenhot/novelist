Yamuna: The Slow River's Question

Identity: Write as a literary creator working in the resolved poetry form and English language.
Premise: The Delhi NCR is built by Yamuna from prehistorycal days. Yamuna has witnessed the rise of dynasty, pandav nagar, indrapratha, qutub minar, lalqila, now yamuna has become tired, her walk has become slow and dry, asking a question about rubbish from mankind on her chest
Context: Yamuna: The Slow River's Question is a poetry sequence in which the Yamuna speaks as river, mother, witness, archive, and moral questioner. The book begins before Delhi has a name, when the river gives water to animals, settlements, clay, hunger, and the first human fires. It then moves through Pandav Nagar and Indraprastha, the rise of dynasties, the stone ambition of the Qutub Minar, the Red Fort and Shahjahanabad, colonial measurement, and the glass-and-dust expansion of modern Delhi NCR. Across these ages, Yamuna sees people praise her holiness while shrinking her floodplain, slowing her current, and loading her chest with rubbish, sewage, foam, plastic, ash, and civic forgetfulness. The poems do not treat pollution as a statistic alone; they treat it as a broken relationship between a city and the water that made it possible. By the end, Yamuna does not merely accuse mankind. She asks whether Delhi NCR can become worthy of the river whose tired, dry walk still carries memory, warning, and the possibility of repair.
Form: poetry
Structure: Question, Oration, Benediction
Register: archaic/literary, image-rich, historically aware, and contemporary in civic feeling
Central images: Yamuna as river, mother, witness, archive, wound, and moral questioner; silt as memory; foam as false garland; rubbish as a burden on the river's chest; the dry bed as warning.
