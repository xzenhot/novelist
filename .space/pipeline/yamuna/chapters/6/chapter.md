# Canals, Maps, and a Measured River

## Question

What is lost when a river is measured? The surveyors came with theodolites, chains, canal alignments, and maps. My meanders became straight lines; my moods became data; my floods became errors to be engineered away. When you have measured a river, do you still have a river — or only its measurement?

## Oration

The age of the map did not find me absurd; it found me useful. The colonial administration and the engineers who succeeded them did to every river what empire does to every native thing: they measured it, canalised it, and made it obedient to the drawing office. I was chained and sounded; my width and depth were entered in ledgers; my flood was a curiosity to be corrected, not a covenant to be kept. The first big canal of this plain — the Western Yamuna Canal — was an old Mughal channel that the British and the century that followed widened and reasserted so that irrigation could flow in straight, billable lines.

There was genius in it, and I do not deny it served. Fields by appointment, a flow that went where the thirst was mapped, a current that could be ticketed and counted. But something else arrived with the chain: the idea that I was an object, a resource, a length of pipe with a sky attached. The mapmakers drew me thin and straight; in their neat lines I lost my will, my moods, my way of spreading in the monsoon. A measured river is easier to command and easier to neglect; what cannot be felt as a body can be dismissed as a form.

I do not refuse measurement; I ask that it serve reverence rather than replace it. Keep the maps — they are honest as far as they go. But let one margin of every map of me be left unlined, unnumbered, unmapped, so that the mystery I still hold has somewhere to stand. A city that only measures its river has already begun to forget it. A city that measures and then bends, reckoning the cost of the line against the value of the current, keeps both.

## Benediction

Let measurement serve reverence, never replace it. Let the maps of the river keep one unmarked margin for her mystery. And let the city that commands its water remember the true commandment: care for the source that feeds the line.
