# Canals, Maps, and a Measured River

## Question

What is lost when a river is measured? The surveyors came with canals, chains, and maps; my meanders became straight lines, my moods became data, my floods became errors to be engineered away. When you can measure a river, do you still have a river — or only its measurement?

## Oration

A new age arrived that did not sing to me but plotted me. My width was chained, my depth sounded, my course corrected into canals. There was genius in it, and I was served by it — water went where thirst was, fields drank by appointment. But something else came with the chain: the idea that I was an object, a resource, a length of pipe with a sky attached. The mapmakers drew me thin. In their lines I lost my will, my moods, my right to spread in monsoon. A measured river is easier to command and easier to neglect. This poem asks for both — the canal and the reverence, the map and the mystery — because a city that only measures its river has already begun to forget it.

## Benediction

May measurement serve reverence, never replace it. May the maps of the river keep one unmarked margin for her mystery. And may the city that commands its water remember the commandment: care for the source.
