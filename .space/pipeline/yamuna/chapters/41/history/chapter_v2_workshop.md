# The Festival and the Aftermath

## Question

What does a river receive on the morning after the festival? The ghats filled with fire and lamps, the water carrying ten thousand flames — then, by dawn, the same bank is a floor of wilted garlands, plastic, and cold ash. Which is the truer image: the festival or the aftermath?

## Oration

I am the great festival's canvas, and I do not refuse it — the light on my water during the holy nights is real, and for a beautiful day I am worshipped as I once was, wholly. But I am also, faithfully, the morning after. The flame that leaves no waste is the honest devotion; the ghat after the dawn is the honest accounting. The offering thrown full into my current — the flowers, the plastic, the cups — becomes, by morning, my hard address. I do not want to forbid the festival; I want the city to love me on the eighth day as much as on the seventh, with the sweeping of the bank and the lifting of the garlands. A river can forgive a festival. A river cannot forgive a festival that forgets the day after. Teach the city: the aftermath is part of the rite.

## Benediction

Let the lamp, and also the clewm, be liturgy. May the bank be swept as reverently as it was lit. And may the river be honored on the morning after, when nothing is bright but the work of care.
