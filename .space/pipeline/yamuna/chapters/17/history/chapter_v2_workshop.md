# Ashes and Return

## Question

Where do the dead return? For millennia the faithful have brought their ashes to my water — the last journey ending in the current, the body becoming river. Now the burning ghats crowd my bank, the smoke thickens the air, half-burned offerings mat my edge. Is return still a sacrament, or has it become another burden?

## Oration

I have received your dead since before your oldest scripture. The ash in my water is not pollution to me; it is the completion of a covenant — from water you came, to water you return. I hold your ancestors. But the covenant had terms the city has forgotten: reverence, proportion, a whole body honored, a fire complete. What arrives now is often haste and economy — half-burned weight, plastic marigolds, the quick disposal of grief. I can carry your dead; I cannot carry your carelessness about them. Let the return be true again: full rites, clean ash, my water touched with the same respect the living once demanded. Then every ending in this city will feed the river that will, in turn, feed its beginning.

## Benediction

May the dead return to the river as guests, not garbage. May grief be given the time and fire it deserves. And may the covenant of ashes be kept so faithfully that the water itself feels honored to receive.
