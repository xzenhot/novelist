# The Archive of Wet Earth

## Question

What does the river's mud remember that no book can? In the wet earth of my banks are the seeds of a thousand years, the layered memories of flood and drought, the pollen of the forests and the chemistry of the ages. What would it mean for the city to read the archive of its wet earth?

## Oration

A book records what a scribe thought worth keeping; my wet mud records what the earth actually did. Layer by layer, in the sediment of my bank, is a truer history than any chronicle: the ash of the ancient fire, the seed of a tree whose forest the plain has forgotten, the raised line where a flood pushed up, the pollen telling of grasses and forests older than Delhi. The city that lets my bank be paved or dredged carelessly is burning its oldest archive without reading the page. Let the city learn to read wet earth as scripture. Before it pours concrete on the flood, let it ask what memory is buried under those feet. The archive can answer, laying out grandfather-floods, the borders of old grasses, the weather of times before histories. Keep the wet archive, city, and you keep the memory of your own ground.

## Benediction

Let the wet bank be read and kept, as the oldest archive on the plain. May the city honor the mud as it honors any scripture, and let no spade come down on a memory yet unread. And may the archive of wet earth, layer by layer, teach the future of a past worth saving.
