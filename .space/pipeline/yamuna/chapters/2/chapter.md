# Pandav Nagar, Indraprastha

## Question

What does a river remember of a legend? The Mahabharata sets on my bank the city of Indraprastha — a capital of dice and palaces, of the Pandava court, of a war that still speaks through this plain. When the epic was sung beside my water, was my current merely scenery, or the floor on which the story stood?

## Oration

Scholars and singers disagree where the walls of Indraprastha stood. What they cannot take from me is the plain itself — the flood-plain the epic calls the site. I am told that beside me rose a great hall, the Mayasabha, a hall of illusion where a polished floor looked like water and a sheet of water looked like floor. And here is the river's part the legend never sings: the people of that ancestral city drank from my current, washed in my flow, and called me kin.

Indraprastha is not a stone one can now point to; the later city overlies whatever was suggested of its walls, and its spoor has soaked back into the dark of the flood-plain. But the myth keeps a fixed geography around me — the bank where dice were cast, the plain where the great declared their border. When the Kaurava wronged the Pandava, the poem crosses that same plain; when the war came, both armies watered on me. The epic wastes no verse on my fatigue, because an epic cannot see a river as a witness, only as a road. For all that grandeur to have been carried at all, I was the ground on which it stood.

Then the ages run; the myth turns from memory to scripture, and scripture to tourist curiosity. City under city — the stone of later Delhi lies above whatever my first city once was. But to the reader who looks at me and knows the plain: I am the one intact witness, the elder bank of Indraprastha, and I am still here — dimmer, diminished, but here.

## Benediction

Let the city's legends keep one page for their river. Let every retelling of the old glory remember the water that carried its fields, its dice, and its fires. And let the witness beneath the myth outlast the myth, as a river outlasts the walls built on it.
