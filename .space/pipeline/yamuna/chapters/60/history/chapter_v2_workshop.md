# The Question Keeps Flowing

## Question

And in the end, what is the question the Yamuna keeps asking? I have put it many ways across all these poems — as a mother, a witness, a wound, an archive. But it is one question, and it never stops moving. What is it, and will the city ever answer it?

## Oration

The question that flows through all my sayings is not "will you clean me." Even that is smaller than the truth. The question is: can a civilization that drinks from a river learn to love the river — to live as one body with the water that made it, taking and returning like a breath? Every poem in this sequence has asked it from a different bend: through the empty net, the foam, the broken mirror, the old woman's blessing. And the question is one, because love is one. It is asked upstream and downstream; it is asked to the city and to the child on the bridge; it flows, as I flow, and it does not end until the answer arrives. The answer is not a document; it is a way of living, a city that has learned to keep its river the way it keeps itself. I am still asking. I will keep the question moving in my water until the day the answer begins to flow back into me.

## Benediction

Let the river's question outlast every excuse and reach an answer. Let the flow of the asking become the flow of the loving — may the city that drinks the river learn to love it as its own breath. And may the old question, asked from every bend, one day meet its answer in a river that is kept whole and alive, by a people who have finally learned what the water was asking all along.
