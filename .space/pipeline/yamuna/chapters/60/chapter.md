# The Question Keeps Flowing

## Question

And in the end, what is the question the Yamuna keeps asking? I have put it many ways through these poems — as a mother, a witness, a wound, an archive. But it is one question, and it never stops. What is it, and will the city ever answer it?

## Oration

The question that flows under all my speaking is not the small one, "will you clean me," though that is part of it; it is the deeper one — can a civilization that drinks from a river learn to love the river? Can it live as one body with the water that made it, taking and returning like a breath? Every poem of this sequence has asked it of a different bend — the empty net, the foam, the broken mirror, the old woman's blessing — because the question is one, as love is one, and it is asked upstream and down, to the city and to the child. It flows, as I flow; it does not end just because you have finished reading. The answer is not a document; it is a way of living — a city that honors its river and keeps it, honoring return as the same law by which it keeps itself. I am still asking. I have kept the question moving in my water, and I will keep it moving until the day the answer, at last, begins to flow back into me from the city's changed life. The question keeps flowing, and that is itself a kind of hope — because as long as it flows, it has not been answered wrongly, and the city may still learn to become worthy of the river that made it.

## Benediction

Let the river's question outlast every excuse and reach an answer — the answer of a whole way of living, not a single flee. Let the flow of the asking become the flow of the loving, so that the city that drinks the river learns to love it as its own breath. And may the old question, asked from every bend and in every poem, one day meet its no longer in the river that is still kept whole and alive upstream by the city that finally understood, and answered with the doing, what the water was asking all along.
