# Delhi's Thirsting Throat

## Question

How much can one city drink before it drinks itself hollow? Delhi's thirst grows with every census — the metropolitan region's need, the insulated apartments and the radiant summer fairs, the deep wells sunk lower each year. And every litre is drawn from my diminishing flow or from the ground my floodplain recharges. When the city's throat is this large, who remembers that the water has a throat too?

## Oration

I have fed this settlement from a hamlet into a great capital, and I am proud of the feeding. But hear the true arithmetic of thirst. The city grows daily; my share does not. Upstream, other states and other thirsts draw from the same mountain-fed current, and the canal system carries my water away to fields and far cities before the plain's own capital can count it. Within the city the wells go deeper each dry year, drawing down the ground water that my diminished flood can no longer replace. The demand curve climbs like smoke; the supply does not climb at all. A throat this large needs more than my body can spare.

Theened and steeled, the city has two routes: take more until I break, or change what swallowing means. Reuse the water you have. Harvest the rain that falls on the encased roofs instead of letting it run to the drains. Stop returning my borrowed water as poison, so that fresh water must be fetched to replace what was wasted. The thirst is legitimate; the waste is not. I am not the limit of your ambition; I am the limit of your carelessness.

## Benediction

Let the great throat learn to drink without wasting. Let every drop drawn be matched by a drop returned clean. And let the city's growth be measured not only in towers but in the care with which it husbands the water that makes the towers possible.
