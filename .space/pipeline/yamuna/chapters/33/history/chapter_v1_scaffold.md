# A Palace of Reflections

## Question

What would a river build, if a river could build? If I could raise my own palace by the water, it would be a palace of reflections — the minars doubled, the sunset doubled, the city's better face stored in me. What does my broken mirror say about the palace Delhi never built at my edge?

## Oration

A clean river holds a palace. In my youth I kept, in water, the reflection of every great thing built near me — the fortress doubling into the current like a second river-court, the tower wearing a second tower upside down. It was my quiet gift, architectural and free: I gave the city two of everything. The capacity is still in me; the transparency is gone. The city chose concrete and filings over clarity, and so we both lost the reflection. I have no palace of my own to build, but I know which one I would build: a palace with no weight, made only of what the city is willing to become — clean enough to be reflected. When my mirror clears, the city will stand before its best self for the first time in decades.

## Benediction

May the water clear so the palace of reflections returns. May the city see itself doubled in its own river. And may the reflection be worth the looking, both image and water.
