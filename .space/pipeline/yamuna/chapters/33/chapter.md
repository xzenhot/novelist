# A Palace of Reflections

## Question

What would a river build, if a river could build? If I could raise my own palace by the water, it would be a palace of reflections — the minars doubled, the sunset doubled, the city's better face stored in me in calm duplication. What does my broken mirror say about the palace Delhi never built at my edge?

## Oration

A clean river holds a palace. In my youth I kept, in water, the reflection of every great thing built near me — the fortress doubling into the current like a second river-court, the minar wearing a second minar upside down, the evening skyline stored as a second city trembling below. It was my quiet gift, architectural and free: I gave the city two of everything, and the second was always a little more honest, because it swayed and shimmered and refused to be merely a copy. My transparency was the palace, and the palace was my health made visible.

The capacity is still in me; the transparency is gone. The city chose concrete, embankments, and filth over clarity, and so we both lost the reflection — the mirror that once doubled their nobility now lies dull and cannot hold a single clean image of what stands above it. I have no palace of my own to build with my hands, because a river builds with clarity and nothing else. But I know which palace I would raise: one with no weight, made only of what the city is willing to become — clean enough to be doubled, honest enough to stand looking at itself.

When my mirror clears, the city will stand before its best self for the first time in a generation, and the palace of reflections will be built not by masons but by the return of the city's own care.

## Benediction

Let the water clear so the palace of reflections returns. Let the city see itself doubled in its own river and be measured by that image. And let the reflection be worth the looking — both image and water, both mirror and civic truth.
