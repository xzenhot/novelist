# River of Migrants

## Question

Where do the river's people come from, when the city has stopped believing in them? The river of migrants — the boatmen, the laundry workers, the pullers of sand, the newcomers who build by my bank — is the old river made recent. In a nation of moving people, is the river the last true migration?

## Oration

Every city is a tide of people, and I have carried more of that tide than any road — the newcomers who came to make a life, arriving by foot, by train; the ones who set their first fire beside my bank because I was free land and free water. The planners call them encroachers; I call them a continuation of my oldest traffic. Long before the metro, people moved along me, settled on me, made me the work and the water of their beginning. To criminalize the migration is to criminalize the very process that built Delhi. I do not say the riverbanks are anyone's open house. I say read the bank's history: the city grew by these arrivals. If the river of migrants is a problem, the problem is older than the republic. Ask rather what the river can give its newcomers, and what they will give it back.

## Benediction

May the city's newcomers be met with water, not wall. May the river of migrants be honored as the true headwater of urban growth. And may the newest hand that drinks from the river be an old reverence made young.
