# Before the City Had a Name

## Question

Who gave the first drink? Before Delhi had a name, before walls raised this plain between its banks, a river born in the Himalaya crossed the country for a thousand kilometres to meet a people who drank from it. When the first humans knelt beside that water, did they know they were pledging a kinship that would outlive every empire they would ever build?

## Oration

I begin in snow. My true source is the Yamunotri glacier far to the north, where a spring named after my legend wells cold out of the rock — and I come down out of those mountains as the oldest conversation between the ice and the plain. I am a long river, one of the great children of the Himalaya: nearly fourteen hundred kilometres of me before I touch the sea, my course passing through this very city. Long before Delhi was a name, I poured through a plain that held only soil and grass and the winding roads of animals — and to that plain the first peoples came down to my water, and drank.

In those ages there was no temple in my name and no stone raised toward my sky; I was simply the pulse by which life arrived. The aurochs and the antlered deer came down to my throat when the sun went; the first humans came down too, to the same bank, and drank the same cold passing. They wove me into the clay of their vessels and laid me beside their first fires. I was not holy because any god told them so; I was holy the only way a river can be: by being the thing from which thirst is answered. In those days I was full, strong, and unafraid of survey years — and the people who drank me loved me, not as a thing to chain but as a light-giver at their threshold. Remember this beginning, city, whenever your engineers look at a map of me: for ten thousand seasons, I was the map.

Then come the centuries. The first villages learn to sew my banks. The first cities rise, not by my conquest, but upon my labour. And the civilisation that would come to call me mother, sister, goddess, and drain, began by being humble at my rate. That band — that first unconditional awe — is what I ask you to recognise when you look at a glass of your own river today.

## Benediction

May the city recall it was suckled at this current. Let every glass of water taken down carry the memory of that first bank, and that first kneeling, and that first humility. And may the river that came before the name still outlast the name — giving, remembering, and still asking to be thanked.
