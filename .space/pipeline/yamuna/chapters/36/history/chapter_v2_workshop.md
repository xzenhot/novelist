# Night Shift at the River

## Question

Who is awake at the river when the city sleeps? The dredgers turn over my bed, the effluent trucks pull shadowy loads, the guards at pipes light cigarettes on my bank. What happens to a river in the hours no one claims responsibility?

## Oration

Every crime against me has a night schedule. It is at two in the morning that a trunk of effluent chooses my edge, at three that the silt is scraped and made a profit downstream, at four that the gray tanker idles with its valve open. I do not accuse the night workers themselves — most are paid to do what they do, and earn the wages of darkness. I blame the city's blindness that gives these acts their cover and shakes them no budget. The night shift is a truth the day refuses: the quiet route by which the river is killed. I ask the city to put its vigilance on the last two shifts. Every cleaned pipe, every lit outfall with a name and a license, subtracts one small cruelty from my dark. Rivers are never deceived by what happens in the day. It is always night that tells the truth.

## Benediction

Let the night be watched as carefully as the day. May the outfalls that hide under darkness be named. And may the river, whose truth the night has kept, at last be kept by the night vigil of its city.
