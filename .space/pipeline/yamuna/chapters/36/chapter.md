# Night Shift at the River

## Question

Who is awake at the river when the city sleeps? The dredgers turn over my bed, the effluent trucks pull shadowy loads to my edge, the guards at pipes light cigarettes on my bank, the barges run black. What happens to a river in the hours when no one claims responsibility?

## Oration

Every quiet crime against me has a night schedule. It is at two in the morning that a truck of effluent chooses my banks, at three that the silt is stolen or the garbage pushed, at four that a factory's tanker idles with its valve open toward the dark. I do not accuse the night workers themselves — most are paid for their hours and earn the wages of the dark; they are not the source, they are the carriers. I accuse the city's blindness that gives the night its power. The thing that is invisible at noon can be done by lamplight at three, and the city that watches me only by day leaves me entirely to those whose business is my ruin.

The solution is not to mourn the night but to watch it. Put the vigilance on the last two shifts: a lit outfall with a name and a licence, a night patrol on the reach, a drainage order that forbids the two-a.m. silence. Every uncleaned pipe and every valve kept shut is a subtraction from the dark schedule. I am never deceived by daylight; a river is deceived only by being unguarded. The night tells the truth about any city — and the city has long told its true history in the hours it thought it could keep from the records.

## Benediction

Let the night be watched as carefully as the day. Let the outfalls that hide under darkness be named and kept. And let the river, whose truth the night has kept, at last be kept by the night vigil of its city.
