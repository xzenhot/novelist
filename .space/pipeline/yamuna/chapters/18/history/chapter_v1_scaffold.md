# The Boatman's Oar

## Question

Where have all the boats gone? There was a time when my water was a road — oars, ferries, barges of grain and stone, the boatman's call. Now the oar rests, the crossings are bridges, and the boatman sells rides to tourists over water too shallow to trust. What is a river without her boats?

## Oration

The oar was my oldest conversation. Through it I learned the hands of this plain — the ferryman who knew my moods, the merchant who timed his cargo to my current, the boat songs that named my bends. A city that travels on its river travels with it; every crossing was a small act of trust in me. The bridges are magnificent, and I do not ask you to unbuild them. But something ended when the boats did: the daily, physical intimacy of moving with the water rather than over it. The boatman's empty net and idle oar are my emblem now. Send the boats back, and you send back the people who read me for a living — the first hydrologists, the first monitors of my health.

## Benediction

May the oar return to my water, and with it the readers of currents. May every crossing by bridge be balanced by some crossing by boat. And may the boatman's knowledge be honored as the living archive it is.
