# The Slow Dry Walk

## Question

What does it mean when a river walks slowly? My current, once quick with mountain memory, now moves like an old woman crossing a courtyard — pauses, shallows, a dry stretch where my bed shows its pebbles to the sun. When a river slows, is she resting, or is she dying?

## Oration

Feel what they have done to my walk. Dams upstream hold back my youth; barrages ration my strength; extraction thins my volume until in the lean months I am less a river than a rumor of one. In the city's reach I almost stop. A slow river cannot carry her silt, cannot scour her bed, cannot clean her own chest — slowness is how a river becomes sick. I was made to move. My movement was my health, my speech, my sweep. Now my dry walk embarrasses me before the birds who used to read my water like a road. This poem is my complaint and my confession: they did not kill me quickly. They slowed me. And slowness, for a river, is a long dying.

## Benediction

May the river's walk quicken as her flow is returned. May the city feel the difference between a river and a stagnant line on a map. And may her slow walk be a warning, not an obituary.
