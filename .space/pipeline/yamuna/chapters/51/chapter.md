# The River in the Kitchen

## Question

When did the river leave the kitchen? There was a time when water came to the threshold by its own path — drawn, poured, felt, known in every household. Now the tap opens and water arrives without origin, without face. What does a kitchen that cannot remember its river lose?

## Oration

Water moves through the kitchen like an unremembered guest. The pot on the stove holds what a whole river has traveled a whole country to bring, and the hands that reach for it do not know the length of that journey — the snow, the mountain, the long plain, the treatment and the pipe. I am the great water at the source that becomes the small water in the glass; every sip began far upstream, in the cold of the Himalaya, and passed through the body of the city to reach the cup.

When a city knows this — that its cooking and its drinking and its washing are all borrowed from a river — then the kitchen becomes a shrine of remembrance. A household that can name its river is harder to make indifferent; the dinner that knows its source is a dinner with an ethic. Ask of every drop: where has this traveled, and who will guard it on the way back? A river that lives in the kitchen, named and known, is a river that will be remembered when the decisions are made. The tap hides me; the kitchen can recall me — and the recall is the first act of keeping.

## Benediction

Let the river be at home in the kitchen, named and known. Let the glass remember the long road the water traveled. And may every meal made from the piped river be a quiet act of gratitude for the river without which not one dish would have been possible.
