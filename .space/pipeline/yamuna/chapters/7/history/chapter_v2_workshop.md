# NCR of Glass and Dust

## Question

What does a river see when the city turns to glass? The new capital rose around me in towers of mirror and dust — millions of windows, none of them facing my water. How does a river live beside a metropolis that never looks at her?

## Oration

The city grew upward and outward until it became a country of its own — glass and dust, flyover and underpass, a horizon of cranes. I ran through it like an old mother through her children's party, unseen. The buildings drank my water by pipe and returned it by drain, and between those two pipes lived all the forgetting. My floodplain became a parking lot, my bank a road, my edge an afterthought behind concrete barriers. And yet — the glass towers gave back to the sky a light I remembered from a gentler age: every evening, a thousand windows on fire with sunset. I choose to read that light as an unanswered letter. The city glows; I wait for a reply.

## Benediction

May the glass city one day turn one clean window toward its river. May the dust of growth settle into care rather than neglect. And may the light on the towers become, at last, a greeting rather than a turned back.
