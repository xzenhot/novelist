# The Fisherman's Empty Net

## Question

When did the fish leave, and who was there to see it? The fisher across the water checks an empty net, hauls it, rechecks it, and goes home to an evening of nothing. It was not sudden; it was the slow graduation of many causes — low oxygen, lost spawning grounds, silt, and the pollution that suffocates the shallows. What is a river without its fish, and who notices a net that comes up empty?

## Oration

I remember when my body could feed the people who lived on me. The fishers of the river were my partners: we shared a grammar of thin days and fat ones, of monsoon runs and dry-case patience, of the knowledge of where the fish would school. Their nets were my health's report card, and for a long age I passed. The fish of my reach — the carp and the catfish and the smaller silvers — were the working wealth of the banks, the protein of the poor.

Then the partnership thinned at the same pace as the fish. The fry lost their grassy nurseries as the reed-beds were cut; the bigger fish lost their oxygen as the effluent load rose and my current slowed; the spawning runs lost their path up the shrinking channel. The net began to come up empty of anything but wet netting, and the fisher's skill — which is intact — found no river to practise it on.

I am hurt for the fisher most of all, because their craft is ancient and their river has betrayed them, though the betrayal was not theirs. The empty net is my honest monitor: the fish are the truth of my health, and they have been schooling downward. When the net fills again, so will my proof that the city has begun to mend. Treat every empty net as a diagnosis, not as one poor man's hard luck, and the recovery will begin where the net hangs.

## Benediction

Let the fisher's net fill again, and the river with them. Let the small fish return before the complaints. And let the oar and the net that live on me be the first sign, and the surest, that I am mine again.
