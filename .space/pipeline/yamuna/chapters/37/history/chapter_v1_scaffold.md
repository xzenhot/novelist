# The Fisherman's Empty Net

## Question

When did the fish leave, and who was there to see it? The fisher across the water now checks an empty net, hauls it, rechecks it. It was not sudden; it was the graduation of many causes — oxygen, spawning grounds, suffocation. What is a river without its fish, and who notices a net that comes up empty?

## Oration

I remember when my body could feed the people who lived on me. The fishers were my partners — we shared a grammar of thin days and fat ones, of monsoon runs and dry patience. Their nets were the report card of my health, and for a long time I passed. Then the partnership thinned at the same pace as the fish: the fry lost their grassy nurseries, the bigger fish lost their oxygen, and the net began to rise empty of anything but a wet abasement. I am hurt for the fishers, whose skill is intact but whose river is not. Their empty net is the truest monitor I have: when it fills again, so will my proof of life. I ask the city to treat every empty net as a read-out with a diagnosis, not a lone poor man's bad luck.

## Benediction

May the fishers' nets fill again, and the river with them. May the small fish return before the complaints. And may the rivers they cast over know that the second they are given is the fate of all that live on me.
