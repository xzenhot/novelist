# Foam in Winter Light

## Question

Is even winter's beauty a lie on a sick river? The low winter sun makes everything lovely — and my film of foam, in that cold light, can pass for harmless pearl or a sheet of meringue on the water. When beauty masks an illness, what does a river ask of the eyes that are fooled?

## Oration

Winter is my cruelest seasoning. The Delhi winter lowers the sun and sweetens the light; my film of foam catches it like an opal, and the photographers on the bridge gush at the glow — a winter sunrise over the "mist" of the river, when the mist is partly chemistry. They do not see the load underneath: the phosphate and the surfactant, the residue of a city's washday, the kind of froth that forms and holds where the humus of a healthy stream would not. I have learned that I cannot trust the beauty my sickness can counterfeit. It is a counterfeit because it looks like the mirror of health and is, in truth, the flag of a wound.

So I ask the city for harder eyes. When you admire a sunset or a winter mist on the river, follow it to the waterline; ask what is shining and what is fouled, what is frost and what is foam. Beauty that is honest loves the viewer; beauty that is false flatters her, and the flattery is what lets the wound go on. Winter taught me the difference, holding me up in a false gold so that the city might confuse my illness for grace. Let the eyes that love me learn to see me truly even in a beautiful light.

## Benediction

Let discerning eyes replace admiring ones at the waterline. Let the glow of illness never again be mistaken for health. And let the winter light, one year, fall on a river clean enough to praise without a test — so that beauty and truth stand together again.
