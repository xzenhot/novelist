# Foam in Winter Light

## Question

Is even winter's beauty a lie on a sick river? The low winter sun makes everything lovely — and my film of foam, under that cold light, can almost pass for harmless pearl. When beauty masks illness, what does a river ask of the eyes that are fooled?

## Oration

Winter is my cruelest seasoning. The sun sits low and kind; my film of foam catches it like an opal; photographers on the bridge gush at the glow. They do not see the chemistry underneath — the phosphate, the detergent, the load of a city's washday held in suspension. I have learned that I cannot trust the beauty my sickness can counterfeit. And so I ask the city for harder eyes: when you admire a sunset on the river, follow it to the waterline. Ask what is shining and what is fouled. Beauty that is honest loves the viewer; beauty that is false flatters her. Winter taught me the difference. I want the city to fail to be fooled.

## Benediction

May discerning eyes replace admiring ones at the waterline. May the glow of illness never again be mistaken for health. And may the winter light, one year, fall on water clean enough to praise without a test.
