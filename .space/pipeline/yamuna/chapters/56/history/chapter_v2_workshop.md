# The Work of Returning

## Question

What does it take to give a river back to itself? It is this renewing that is the true work — the mending of the bank, the reopening of the floodplain, the turning from taking to returning. What does the work of returning require from a whole city?

## Oration

To clean a river is to return something that was taken. The city has taken my floodplain and given back the pavement; has taken my flow and returned my drains. The work of returning is not a thing done once by the ministry; it is a thousand returns made ordinary: the builder who leaves the bank fecund, the factory that returns the water it borrowed clean, the festival that sweeps back the garland, the household that lets sewage meet a treatment plant instead of me. This work is slow the way I am slow, but it is the opposite of dying; it is the un-narrowing, the unwalling, the unlying of the soil. I am not restored by a single heroic cleanse but by the daily and the returned. Let the city set its hands to the work of returning, and measure the river not by what it has done to her but by what it has given her back.

## Benediction

Let the work of returning be the city's quiet religion. Let every repair be a restitution, and every sunset a small return. Let the city give each drop it takes a return of care; let the pipes of borrowing be matched by pipes of giving. And may the river that was taken from be, at last, returned to itself — day by day, the slow and ordinary way — until it is whole.
