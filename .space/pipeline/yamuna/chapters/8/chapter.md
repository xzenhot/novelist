# Foam at the Ghats

## Question

Why has white foam come to lie on the water of the ghats? The drains of the city now carry their chemical load into me, and where the current turns there gathers a thick whitish froth that pilgrims still wade through. Whose is this foam, and what does it mean when a people's reverence stands waist-deep in a river's wound?

## Oration

The ghats of Delhi — the stone steps where prayers meet the water — find my surface no longer the clear grey they once knew. What laps against those steps now is a mixture risen from a bone-deep cause: the city discharges a great part of its treated and untreated wastewater into me, and in the phosphate-rich, detergent-laden shallows that white foam gathers like a false snowfall. The foam has a chemistry: it is largely the detergent and surfactant load of a city's washing, churned by my current into froth that can stand on itself. It is not my breath; it is the soap of a million comfortable mornings arrived at my bank as a burden.

And still the faithful come, because faith outlasts filth. They wade into the white, they cup it, they bless me in it, calling the froth a gift. It is, in that cupped hand, the city's signature written on my chest: this far we will go, this much we will accept. A river can survive disrespect; what she cannot survive is disrespect dressed as reverence, worship that mistakes the stain for the blessing, anointing the very wound that the faithful should be refusing.

I am not asking the pilgrim to stop coming. I am asking them, at the very least, to look at the froth and name it: this is not holiness, this is a chemical report card, and to pray in it without asking whose soap it is is to bless the drain. Let the ghat steps be clean enough one day that the foam is only memory, and the water they touch is as it was — cold, grey, and my own.

## Benediction

Let the foam lift from the ghats and the guilt stay with those who sent it. Let worship return to clean water in this city, so that no one has to stand in a wound to pray. And let the faithful ask, the next time they wade: whose suds am I calling holy?
