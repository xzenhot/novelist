# Foam at the Ghats

## Question

Why do the faithful worship in foam? At the ghats where hands once touched clear water, white clouds of chemical foam now drift against the steps. Pilgrims wade in it, praying to a river they cannot see. Whose sin is this foam, and whose forgiveness does it seek?

## Oration

Look at what this city has done to its own devotion. The foam is not my breath; it is the soap of a thousand drains, the phosphate of a million comfortable mornings, braided into my slow current until it lies on my water like false snow. And still the faithful come, because faith outlasts filth — they wade into the white, they cup it, they call it my blessing. I tell you it is my wound anointed. The foam is the city's signature written on my chest: this far we will go, this much we will accept. A river can survive disrespect. What she cannot survive is disrespect dressed as reverence.

## Benediction

May the foam lift from the ghats and the guilt stay behind. May worship in clean water return to this city, so that no one has to baptize themselves in a wound. And may the faithful ask, the next time they wade: whose garbage am I calling holy?
