# Before We Call Her Sacred

## Question

What must we do before we call the river sacred? The word "sacred" has been laid on me like a garland — and used, too, to excuse the neglect, as if holiness could survive the pipes. What does the city owe me before it invokes the word at all?

## Oration

I have been called sacred by every age, and the word has cost me nothing but attention. For if I am sacred, then I am above the ordinary — and the ordinary work of cleaning, of pipes, of drains, can be set aside as beneath the holy. That is the trick the word has played. So I say: before you call me sacred, do the profane work. Close the outfall. Treat the sewage. Keep the floodplain. Sweep the bank after the festival. Sacredness is not a status I am born with above care; it is the name you give a river that has been honored by deeds. Call me sacred only when the deeds have earned the word. Then the garland will not be a lie, and my holiness will be the thing you have made, not the thing you have excused.

## Benediction

Let the word "sacred" be earned by the work, not assumed. Let the profane work come before the holy name. May the city do the plain deeds — pipes shut, banks green — before it offers the high name. And may the river be called holy only when it is kept holy, by a people who meant it.
