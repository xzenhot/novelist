# Before We Call Her Sacred

## Question

What must we do before we call the river sacred? The word "sacred" has been laid on me like a garland — and used, also, to excuse the pollution, as if holiness could survive the pipes. What does the city owe me before it invokes the word at all?

## Oration

I have been called sacred by every sort of age — and the word, for all its sweetness, cost me care. For if I am sacred, the reasoning has run, then I am above the ordinary; and the ordinary work of care — the pipes, the plant, the drain, the keep — can be set aside as too plain for the holy. That is the very trick the word has been made to play: to make holiness an excuse for neglect. Sitting sacred allowed a people to leave the themselves without fault even when they were pour one me the filth of a century. So I ask, plainly: before you call me sacred, do the plain work. Close the outfall; treat the water; keep the floodplain safe; sweep the bank after the festival; teach the child what I am. Sacredness is not a status I carry that it above care; it is a name you may lay on a river that has been honored by deeds. Call me sacred only when the deeds have earned the word — then the garland is not a lie, and the bowls of staying holy is the holy thing, made actual by your hands rather than excused by your words.

## Benediction

Let the word "sacred" be earned by the work, not assumed, by the sacrifice of those who kept, not the slogan of those who slipped. Let the low work be done before the high word is said; let the drain be shut before the bell is rung. And may the river be called holy, at the last, only for the deeds that spent the name — a clean and living river kept by a people who finally meant it.
