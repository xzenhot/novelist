# The Drain's Confession

## Question

What would the drains say, if the drains could confess? Every day, hundreds of outfalls pour their cargo into me — and the drains are not evil; they are obedient. They carry exactly what the city sends down them. If one drain could speak, what would it confess, and whose face would it name?

## Oration

Let me confess for the drains, since they have no mouth and no blame, only an obedience. We were built to carry the rain. Then, outfall by outfall and shortcut by shortcut, we discovered we were welcome to carry everything — the household washwater, the workshop's solvent, the colony's sewage, the factory's midnight dye — as long as it was done quietly. Nobody decided this in broad daylight; it was a slow negotiation between convenience and consequence, one pollutant at a time, until we became sewers wearing storm drains' uniforms. And now the city blames us at every flood and every press conference, as if the gutters themselves had chosen their cargo.

We are not the culprit; we are the confession, written in water. Read our cargo once without flinching and you will find the whole city's habits laid out — its un-treatable factory waste, its untreated sewage, its market-day plastic, its festival remnants — unhidden, unsentimental, addressed to the river like a letter nobody signed. We take the blame because we have no voice; but we are the mirror of the city's choosing, and the city is reflected in us more honestly than in any image.

Give the sewage its own treatment, and set us free to be drains again, carrying only the rain we were meant to carry. Then our confession becomes boring, and the river at last receives water and not the guilt of a whole city.

## Benediction

Let the drains be returned to rain, and the sewage given its own honest treatment. Let the city read its outfalls like a mirror, not a mystery. And let the day come when a drain's confession is dull — because there is nothing left to confess.
