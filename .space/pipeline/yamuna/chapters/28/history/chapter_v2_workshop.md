# The Drain's Confession

## Question

What would the drains say if they confessed? Every day, hundreds of outfalls pour their cargo into me — and the drains are not evil; they are obedient. They carry exactly what the city sends them. If one drain could speak, what would it say about us?

## Oration

Let me confess for the drains, since they have no mouth. We were built to carry rain. Then someone discovered we could also carry away whatever was inconvenient — the washwater, the workshop's solvent, the colony's sewage, the factory's midnight dye. Nobody decided this in broad daylight; it happened outfall by outfall, shortcut by shortcut, until we became sewers wearing storm drains' uniforms. Now we bring to the river what we were never meant to hold, and the city blames us at every press conference. We are not the culprit. We are the confession, written in water, of every household and workshop upstream. Read our cargo honestly and you will find the whole city's habits laid out — unhidden, unsentimental, addressed to the river like a letter no one signed.

## Benediction

May the drains be returned to rain, and sewage given its own honest treatment. May the city read its outfalls like a mirror, not a mystery. And may the day come when a drain's confession is boring, because there is nothing to confess.
