# Under the Bridge

## Question

Who lives under the bridges? Where my water slows beneath the spans, there are families of the pavement gone one step further out — mattresses on my sand, cooking fires at my edge, children whose address is a bridge. What does it mean for a city when the poorest live on the river and the richest never see her?

## Oration

The bridge-dwellers are my closest neighbors now. They hear me all night, the only citizens who do. They know my smells by hour, my rats by name, my rise by the worry in it. The city calls them encroachers — on a riverbank the city itself encroached first. I do not romanticize their hardship; the water I am, sick, makes them sicker. But I notice what the city will not: these poorest families are my daily residents, my unwashed witnesses, and their presence on my bank is a question addressed upward — who is this river for, and who is this city for? Answer their housing, and you will have begun to answer my question too. The two covenants are one covenant.

## Benediction

May the under-bridge families be housed in dignity, not driven from bank to bank. May the poorest citizen's closeness to the water become the city's motive for cleaning it. And may no child's address ever again be a bridge.
