# A Mirror with No Face

## Question

What does a river mirror when she is too dirty to reflect? I was Delhi's mirror — forts, minarets, festivals, skies doubled in my current. Now the surface lies dull and fouled, and the city looks in and sees nothing but itself as a smear. What does a people lose when the water stops returning their image?

## Oration

A clean river is the city's honest mirror. In me, Shahjahanabad saw its domes doubled; the minar saw itself twice, once in stone and once in water; the fireworks of a hundred festivals bloomed twice, once above and once below. Reflection was my second gift, after water — the gift by which a city saw itself not as earth but as a being, lifted and returned in the current. It is a consolation the city nightly needed: the standing beauty made moving, the skyline rendered double because the water was clear enough to hold it.

Now my surface lies scummed and streaked, and the mirror has no face. The city watching the polluted surface sees no doubling of its towers, only a dull break and shine that refuses the image. This is not merely the loss of a view; it is the loss of a daily practice of self-regard. A people who cannot see themselves doubled in their river cannot recognize what they have become, because the water gave them back the truth of their own built self with a calm no photograph could.

I do not threaten in this; I describe. Clean me, and I will show you your skyline and your conscience once more — the straight tower and the bent citizen, both upright and reflected. Mirrors reward those who clean them. The face they return is the one the cleaner was willing to deserve.

## Benediction

Let the mirror be cleaned, and the face return. Let the city see itself doubled in its river — skyline above, skyline below, both worth the reflection. And let the image it finds there be one it can live with and keep.
