# A Mirror with No Face

## Question

What does a river mirror when she is too dirty to reflect? I was Delhi's mirror — forts, minarets, festivals, skies. Now the surface lies dull with film, and the city looks in and sees nothing. What happens to a people who lose their reflection?

## Oration

A clean river is the city's honest mirror. In me, Shahjahanabad saw its domes doubled; the minar saw itself twice, once in stone and once in water; the fireworks of a hundred festivals bloomed twice. Reflection was my second gift, after water. Now my surface is scummed and streaked, and the mirror has no face. The city has not merely lost a view; it has lost the daily practice of self-regard. A people who cannot see themselves in their river will not recognize what they have become. I say this without cruelty: clean me, and I will show you your skyline again — and also, if you look carefully, your conscience. Mirrors reward those who clean them.

## Benediction

May the mirror be cleaned, and the face return. May the city see itself doubled in its river — skyline above, skyline below, both worth the reflection. And may the image it finds there be one it can live with.
