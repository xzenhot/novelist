# Children on the Bridge

## Question

What do children see from a bridge that their parents have stopped seeing? They lean over the rail, look down at the grey water, and laugh or wrinkle their noses — the river as spectacle, or smell. When did a mother become a view?

## Oration

From the bridge I am scenery, and scenery is easy to poison. The children look down and see no river of legend, no mother of Indraprastha — only a slow brown line that smells of drains. Their parents call that childhood memory; I call it theft. For each child who learns that a river smells like this, a future voter, engineer, priest, or poet is formed with the wound built in. But I watch longer than despair allows. Some children keep looking. Some trace my curve and ask why it shines at one bend and stinks at another. From those questions, everything can still be rebuilt. A child on a bridge is the city's conscience in training — if the elders answer honestly.

## Benediction

May the children who look down be told the truth about the water. May their questions shame the responsible into work. And may the next generation cross that bridge over a river they are proud to show.
