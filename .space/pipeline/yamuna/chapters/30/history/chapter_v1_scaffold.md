# The Engineer's Notebook

## Question

What does the river look like in an engineer's notebook? Columns of discharge, extraction, BOD, rainfall. Precise, honest, and completely silent about the mother. Can the numbers be made to serve the river instead of replacing her?

## Oration

I do not hate the notebook; I live in it now. The data is true — the falling discharge, the rising coliform, the shrinking flow between barrages. What is missing is the reader's heart. Numbers were meant to be my pulse chart, taken so the patient could be healed. Instead they became my epitaph, updated annually. So I make the engineers an offer, mother to technician: keep your columns, but add one more — a line for what the measurement *demands*. Every figure must end in an action, dated and owned. A reading of filth that produces no cleaning is not science; it is paperwork about a murder. The notebook can save me. It has simply been asked the wrong question: not *how sick is she*, but *what will you do by Friday*.

## Benediction

May every number in the notebook end in a deed. May the engineers remember the patient is a mother. And may the day come when the most urgent line in the book is: healed — maintain.
