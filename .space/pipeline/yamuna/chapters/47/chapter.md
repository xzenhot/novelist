# The City Learns to Bend

## Question

Can a city learn to bend around a river, the way a river bends around the land? The straight road, the straightened bank, the channel made obedient — all of it was the geometry of control, the belief that a river's meander is waste. What would it look like for the city to give way to my curve instead of forcing me to its line?

## Oration

I have bent around a hundred obstacles in my long journey, and the lesson of the curve is this: to flow is to yield, and to yield is to keep. The channel that meanders drops its silt, feeds its edges, holds its water longer, and gives its plain the slow green wealth a straight line never can. The city, by contrast, builds straight — the embankment that does not give, the channel that does not meander, the line drawn on the map as final truth. Straightness, for a river, is a kind of death: a straightened river runs too fast, drops its silt too late, and starves the very plain that made it necessary.

I am not asking the city to tear down its embankments; that is not realistic and I know it. I am asking it to learn the bend — to give back, on one reach, a curve of floodplain, to let one meander be a meander again, to treat the river's line not as an error but as a design. A city that bends around its river builds twice — once for the straight line and once for the curve — but it builds a river that lasts and a city that will not drown in its own straightness. The geometry of the river, patient and yielding, is the geometry the city now needs.

## Benediction

Let the city learn the bend. Let one curve be restored to a straightened bank. And let the geometry of the river — patient, yielding, alive — teach the geometry of the city to give a little, so that it may hold for a long time.
