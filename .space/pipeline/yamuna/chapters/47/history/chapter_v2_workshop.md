# The City Learns to Bend

## Question

Can a city learn to bend around a river, the way a river bends around the hills? The straight road, the straightened bank, the channel made obedient — all of it was the geometry of control. What would it look like for the city to give way to my curve instead?

## Oration

I have bent around a hundred obstacles in my long journey, and the lesson of the curve is this: to flow is to negotiate. The city, by contrast, builds straight — the embankment that does not yield, the channel that does not meander, the map that will not bend. And straightness, for a river, is a kind of death. A straightened river runs too fast, drops her silt too late, and starves the plain that a slow curve would feed. I am not asking the city to unbuild its embankments; I am asking it to learn the bend: to give, on one reach, a curve of the flodplain back to me, to let one meander be a meander again. A city that bends around its river builds twice — once for the line, once for the curve; but it builds a river that lasts.

## Benediction

Let the city learn the lovely bend. Let one curve be restored to the straightened bank. And may the geometry of the river — patient, yielding, live — teach the geometry of the city to yield a little, and so to hold longer.
