# The Snowmelt Memory

## Question

What does a river remember of its mountains? Somewhere far to the north, in the snows of the Himalaya, I begin — at the Yamunotri glacier where a spring wells out of the rock — and all that long descent carries the memory of ice. The city drinks from my plain without knowing the mountain is still in the water. Does the source still live in the glass?

## Oration

The surveyors chart me from the map; the pilgrims know me from the myth; but only the far walkers know me from the source. I begin at the Yamunotri glacier in the Uttarakhand Himalaya, where the melt gathers into my first cold thread and begins the long drop: past the shrine and the forest, gaining the mountain tributaries, gathering the headwaters until I am a river of the plain bearing a mountain's watermark. My water, in that first stretch, is dammed, diverted, shared — the mountain feds the canal that waters fields a country away. The memory of snow never fully leaves my body.

This is the claim the city forgets when it sees me as a drain: purity is not a myth about me, it is my upstream self, waiting in solution. The water that arrives at the tap began in the cold of the glacier and passed through the whole history of the plain. The capacity for my restoration is therefore not alien to me; it is in my memory. A river that once ran clear can be made clear again, because the source is still in the current.

Restore my flow and you restore my walk; restore my walk and the snowmelt memory carries the whole system home. Let the city taste, in one honest glass of clean water, the mountain. Let it understand that every drink is a reunion with a glacial source a people will never see but can always honor. Repair is possible not because it is wished but because the river still remembers what it is.

## Benediction

Let the city taste the mountain in its glass. Let every act of cleaning be called by its true name — returning the river to her source-self. And let the snowmelt memory never be fully drowned, for in it lives the proof that repair is not a hope but a return.
