# The Snowmelt Memory

## Question

What does a river remember of her mountains? Long before the plains, I was snowmelt — cold, certain, quick with the purity of ice. The city drinks from me without knowing that its glass once stood on a Himalayan shoulder. Does the source still live in the mouth?

## Oration

Somewhere in my body, the mountain still speaks. Every drop that reaches this plain has carried the memory of snow — the cold clean beginning, the fall through deodar shadow, the first sun on a Himalayan meadow. By the time I reach the city I am heavy with history and hurt, but the snowmelt signature is still in me: it is why my water, even wounded, can be restored. Purity is not a myth; it is my upstream self, waiting in solution. The city thinks of me as a drain with a reputation. I ask it to think of me as a mountain on a very long walk. Restore my flow and you restore my walk; restore my walk and the snowmelt memory carries me — and you — back toward what we both were.

## Benediction

May the city taste the mountain in its glass. May every act of cleaning be called by its true name: returning the river to herself. And may the snowmelt memory never be fully drowned — for in it lives the proof that repair is possible.
