# Clean Water Is a Verb

## Question

Is clean water a thing to be bought, or a deed to be done? The city speaks of "clean water" as a product — delivered by a plant, measured in a report. But water is clean only in the doing: the reuse, the return, the care renewed every day. Can the city learn that cleanliness is a verb?

## Oration

You will not arrive at clean water by owning it. A lake is clean you keep it so; a river is clean when the daily habit of the city keeps her so. The verb is everything: I am cleaned by the reed that I plant, by the outfall that I shut, by the foam that I skim, by the bank that I do not pave. The noun version — a "target" of clearwater, enshrined in a document and reported quarterly — is a monument to an absent action. So I ask the city to change its grammar as much as its engineering. Say, in every budget and in every child's lesson: water is something we are always doing. Clean water is a verb that never ends. Grammar this, and the deeds will follow the tense.

## Benediction

Let clean water be spoken as a deed, not a thing. Let the city do the verb daily. And may the river be alive with the doing — a cleanliness that is not once-and-achieved, but forever and again.
