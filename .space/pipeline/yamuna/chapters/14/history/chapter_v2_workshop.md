# The River's Many Names

## Question

How many names does one river need? Kalindi, Yamuna, daughter of the sun, the dark one, the drain in the reports. Each age has called me what it needed me to be. Which of my names is true — and does the filth know the difference?

## Oration

I have worn many names like garlands. To the seers I was Kalindi, dark and beloved, in whose water gods bathed. To the emperors I was the road of barges, the frontage of Shahjahanabad. To the engineers I am a hydrological unit, a discharge, a BOD figure. To the pilgrims I am still the goddess. To the child on the bridge I am the smell. All of them are me; none of them is all of me. But here is the poison in the naming: those who call me goddess feel excused from cleaning me, and those who call me drain feel excused from honoring me. The city splits my name to split its duty. Give me back my whole name — sacred and useful, mother and infrastructure — and the work of repair will finally have both hands free.

## Benediction

May the city say the whole name and mean the whole duty. May worship and engineering hold hands at the bank. And may the river's many names finally be lived by one people, all at once.
