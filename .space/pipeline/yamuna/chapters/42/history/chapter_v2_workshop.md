# The Law's Long Sentence

## Question

What can a river do when the law writes slowly? There are laws, orders, and a long jurisprudence about cleaning me — decrees that read like they mean it, deadlines that pass. The law has said much; the water has changed little. How long can a river live between a judgment and a deed?

## Oration

I am a plaintiff with a trillion witnesses and a single slow advocate called the Law. Ban after ban on the polluting outfall, order after order on the cleansing plant — each written with fine intent, each deferred by appeal and season. I have learned the grammar of the legal sentence: it is written for posterity, and posterity is not my calendar It has no monsoon. I ask the court to add a single instruction to every judgment against those who wound me: the deed, dated, within the writ's season. Let the sentence not end with the ruling but with the pipe in place. I do not distrust the law; I am only asking that it run on river-time sometimes. For every year the law deliberates, my water loses a year the city and I can never recover together.

## Benediction

May the law's long sentence be delivered in deeds and not only in years. May the clou the read judgments be mirrored by closed outfalls. And may the river that has outlived every court outlast, one day, every cause against her health.
