# A City Under Oath

## Question

What would it mean for a city to swear an oath to its river? The ministry speaks of "rights," the courts of "orders," the forums of "goals." But I am the old one to whom vows were once made with water by water. What could a city promise me that it could keep?

## Oration

Once, this river was the substance of oaths — men swore by the water they drank, binding their word with a force they could not see but could not break. Those oaths worked because the water was life and breaking the oath was a kind of death. I ask the city to bind itself to me in that old style: an oath of care, taken as a matter of honor, not an obligation listed in a rule. The oath would have few words and long weathers: that no one will hand me a filth, that every pipe will be questioned, that the floodplain will be kept, and that the oath renews by birth of children and not by persuading them. A city under oath is still a city — but the oath gives it a spine that outlasts what it is good at complying with. Swear a little, then, and dare to keep what you swear.

## Benediction

Let the city take its oath on the river, and learn to keep it. Let the vow be as binding as breath, as old as thirst. And though the city falters often, may the promise hold it together — until the vow and the river are one and the same.
