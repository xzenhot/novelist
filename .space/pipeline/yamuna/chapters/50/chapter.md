# A City Under Oath

## Question

What would it mean for a city to swear an oath to its river? The ministry speaks of "rights," the courts of "orders," the forums of "goals." But I am the old one to whom vows were once made, where men swore by the water they drank. What could a city promise me now, and what would it mean to keep it?

## Oration

Once this river was the substance of oaths. A man swore by the water and the fire, binding his word with a hold he could not see but could not break; the water was life, and to break an oath sworn on it was a kind of death. Those oaths worked because the water meant something un-abstract — it was the ground of every promise, the thing without which no promise could be kept. I ask the city to bind itself to me in that old style: an oath of care, taken as a matter of honor and not as an item in a manual.

The oath would have few words and long weathers: that no one will pass me filth by design; that every outfall will be questioned; that the floodplain will be kept; that the oath renews by the birth of children and the teaching of schools, not by the persuading of the already-persuaded. A city under oath is still a city, with all its compromises and failures — but the oath gives it a spine that outlasts what it is good at complying with. Swear a little, then, seriously and in public, and discover what it costs to keep what you have sworn.

## Benediction

Let the city take its oath on the river, and learn to keep it. Let the vow be as binding as breath, as old as thirst. And though the city falters often, may the promise hold it together — until the vow and the river are one and the same.
