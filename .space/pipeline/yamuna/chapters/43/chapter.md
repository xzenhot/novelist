# The Woman Who Plants Reeds

## Question

Who is the truest healer of the riverbank? Not the engineer with a blueprint, not the ministry with a scheme — but the woman who plants reeds, kneeling in my mud where no one watches. What is a single act of planting against an injury as broad as a city's neglect?

## Oration

She comes when the bank is quiet, the reed-planting woman, a trowel and a green certainty in her hands. The reeds she sets will filter what the pipes will not, hold my bank where the rubble would not, give the fry a nursery, and quiet the water a little. They are small the way seeds are small, and unpaid, and uncelebrated. The ministry has budgets taller than her knees and committees broader than her plot, and yet her small green action does what the committees only describe: it improves me by one root, genuinely, without a single objection and without a single office.

I am the receiver of the smallest and oldest medicine on this plain — the gardener's hand returning to the river it drank from. I do not claim she can save me alone; no one can. I claim only that the city's real restoration is not one great dam but a thousand such knees and trowels, multiplied until the bank is a rooting of living things. Honor the planters; copy the planters; fund the planters. The reed is the river's staff, and it grows only where a patient hand sets it. The woman who plants reeds is the ministry the ministry has not yet created.

## Benediction

Let the reed-planters multiply, and the reeds with them. Let the smallest act of planting be counted among the city's great works. And let the river learn, root by root, that its healing is the multiplication of one kneeling hand.
