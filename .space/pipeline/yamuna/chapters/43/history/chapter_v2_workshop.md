# The Woman Who Plants Reeds

## Question

Who is the truest healer of the riverbank? Not the engineer with a blueprint, not the official with a scheme — but a woman who plants reeds, kneeling in my mud where no one watches. What is a single act of planting against a wound as broad as a city's neglect?

## Oration

She comes when the bank is quiet, a trowel in one hand and a stubborn tenderness in the other. The reeds will filter what the pipes will not, hold my bank where the rubble would not, shelter the little fish that will, in their turn, shelter the birds. Her work is small the way a seed is small, unpraised and unpaid. The ministry has budgets taller than her and committees broader; she has a root and a green conviction. And yet she has begun a healing none of the meetings can begin: from the bottom, by hand, for the love of it.

I am the receiver of the smallest and oldest medicine on this plain — the gardener's hand returned to the river it drank from. I will not claim her alone can save me. I claim this: the city's real restoration is not one grand dam, but ten thousand such kneelers multiplied until the bank stirs with life again. Honor the planters. Copy the planters. The rain will do the rest.

## Benediction

Let the reed-planters multiply, and the reeds with them. Let the hand that plants a reed be counted among the city's great engineers. And may the river that is healed root by root teach the city that large change is, most often, small love repeated.
