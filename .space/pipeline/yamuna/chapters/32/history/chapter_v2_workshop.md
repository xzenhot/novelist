# The Plastic Prayer

## Question

What does a city pray with, when it prays at a river? Plastic flowers, packaged offerings, prayer flags in synthetic colors. The devotion is sincere; the vessel is eternal garbage. Can a prayer offered in plastic be heard, or only be believed?

## Oration

The garland the pilgrim throws is no longer woven of jasmine; it is plastic, came from a shop a mile away, bought to be thrown. The diya floats on a polystyrene tray. The holy water is poured from a container that will outlive the temple. I do not doubt the heart behind it — the heart is old, and it is the only part that has not changed. But the wrapping has become the prayer, and the wrapping will be in my silt for four centuries. I ask the faithful to burn one small incense of the old kind: to bring what returns to water. A leaf boats on me fine. A clay diya sinks and becomes mud again. Pray with materials that, like the river, are willing to give themselves back.

## Benediction

May the prayers thrown be prayers that return. May devotion choose the leaf over the plastic, the rose over the polymer. And may the river be honored by offerings that agree, like her, to become something else.
