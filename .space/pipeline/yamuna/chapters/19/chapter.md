# Monsoon's Forgotten Door

## Question

Where does the monsoon enter the city now? Once my rise was the monsoon's door — the water spreading wide over the floodplain, filling the ponds and the wells, the year's great renewal arriving on schedule. Now the door is bricked with concrete, and the same rain runs off the rooftops into drowned streets. Who closed the door, and who will unbuild it?

## Oration

The monsoon and I were always one event, one arrival. My crest was the door by which the year's water entered the land: it spread over my floodplain, soaked into the ground, recharged the wells that would be drawn all the dry year, and rolled the fields green. The old city knew the rhythm and built with it — settlement on the high bank, wells at the margin, care for my spread. The arrival was a guest, welcomed.

The modern city dismantled the arrival. It paved my entry, channelled my spread into drains sized for a harmless drizzle, and then expressed astonishment and grief when the water, with nowhere else sanctioned to go, goes where it has always gone when blocked — into the streets. Every flooded road is me knocking at a door I was promised but which was bricked over. Every drowned underpass is the monsoon arriving where it was told, because the door where I lived was removed.

This is not fate; it is geometry. Reopen my floodplain and the monsoon enters as guest rather than intruder: my spread becomes your recharge, my crest becomes your calendar again. The door was never locked by the river. It was locked by a city that stopped believing in water's own schedule. Unbuild the false door, and the true one opens.

## Benediction

Let the monsoon's door stand open — floodplain unbuilt, channels breathing. Let the rains be welcomed as the year's renewal, framed as a guest, not feared as an accident. And let the city learn, as it once knew, to live on the water's schedule instead of against it.
