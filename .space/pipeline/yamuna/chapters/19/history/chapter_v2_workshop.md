# Monsoon's Forgotten Door

## Question

Where does the monsoon enter the city now? Once my floods were the monsoon's door — water spreading wide over the plain, filling every pond and well, the year's great renewal. Now the door is bricked shut, and the rain runs off concrete into drowned streets. Who closed the door, and who will reopen it?

## Oration

The monsoon and I were always one event. My rise was the year's opening — fields sown, wells charged, festivals timed to my crest. The city has dismantled that arrival. It paved my entry, channeled my spread into drains sized for a drizzle, and then expressed astonishment when water finds its own door. Every flooded street is me, knocking. Every drowned underpass is the monsoon arriving where you built, because you removed the door where I lived. Reopen my floodplain and the monsoon enters as guest, not intruder: my spread becomes your recharge, my crest becomes your calendar again. The door was never locked by the river. It was locked by a city that stopped believing in water's own schedule.

## Benediction

May the monsoon's door stand open — floodplain unbuilt, channels breathing. May the rains be welcomed as the year's renewal, not feared as an accident. And may the city learn to live on water's schedule, as it did for a thousand years.
