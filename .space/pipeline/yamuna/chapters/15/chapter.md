# A Handful of Water

## Question

What can a single palm hold of a river? The pilgrim cups me; the farmer lifts a palmful to the seed; the child splashes and is gone. Between the handful and the whole river lies the whole mystery of need, thirst, and enough. Can a city that has learned to think in pipelines learn again to think in handfuls?

## Oration

I am a river of great length and many purposes — and yet I am known, most truly, in handfuls. The bridal handful at the rite, the dying man's last wet spoonful, the farmer's measured palm at the sowing, the first handful a thirsty child takes before anyone has taught them to name me — these are my truest measures. A handful teaches the first ethic of water: take what the palm can hold and let the rest flow on. It is the smallest unit of reverence, and the only one that scales honestly, because the hand cannot take more than it can drink and must give the palm's excess back.

The pipe replaced the handful and took the honesty with it. A pipeline has no palm, no visible limit, no modest refusal; it draws what it is ordered to draw and is gone, and the taking becomes both invisible and endless. The city that drinks through pipe has stopped touching the water it uses, and what it does not touch it does not feel, and what it does not feel it wastes. This is not sentiment; it is the arithmetics of care. When every household falls back, at least once, into the sensation of actually drawing water — of feeling its weight, its cold, its presence — the city will measure its thirst in handfuls again.

I do not ask for a return to the tedious labour of hand-drawing. I ask for the return of the *awareness* the hand gave: that every glass is a river reduced. Teach the child the handful before you teach them the tap, and you will have built the whole ethic of water on the palm.

## Benediction

Let every hand that draws water feel the river in it. Let the city measure its thirst in handfuls before it measures it in billions of litres. And let the smallest unit of taking carry the smallest unit of thanks, so that the pipe never outrides the palm.
