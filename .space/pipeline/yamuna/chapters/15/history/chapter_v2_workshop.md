# A Handful of Water

## Question

What can a single hand hold of a river? A pilgrim cups my water; a farmer lifts a palmful to seed; a child splashes and runs. Between the handful and the whole river lies the whole story of need, thirst, and taking. Can a city learn to think in handfuls again?

## Oration

I am vast, yet I am known in handfuls. The bride's handful in the farewell rite, the dying man's handful at the last rite, the farmer's measured handful at the seed — each one a small covenant with me. Handfuls taught humility: take what the palm holds, return the rest. The city replaced the handful with the pipeline, and pipelines have no palms — no visible measure, no limit the hand can feel. This is how taking became invisible and therefore endless. I ask the city to teach its children the handful again: draw water, feel its weight, know that every glass is a river reduced. The handful is the smallest unit of reverence and the only one that scales honestly.

## Benediction

May every hand that draws water feel the river in it. May the city measure its thirst in handfuls before it measures it in gallons. And may the smallest unit of taking come with the smallest unit of thanks.
