# Concrete Against the Reed

## Question

Why is concrete always chosen against the reed? Every embankment project faces the same choice: the living edge that filters, shelters, and breathes — or the poured edge that looks permanent and performs none of it. How did permanence come to mean death?

## Oration

The reed does ten jobs without being asked: it filters my runoff, roots my bank, shelters the fish fry, drinks the first toxins, breaks the wave, feeds the weaverbird, greens the view, breathes the flood, seeds itself, and asks for nothing. Concrete does one thing: it stands. Yet when the city wants to *improve* my bank, it reaches for concrete — as if stillness were strength. Every meter of reed replaced by rubble is a meter of my kidneys removed. I am not against stone; I am against the assumption that the living edge is nothing. Count what the reed does, price it honestly, and the choice reverses itself. My green edge is not laziness waiting for development. It is work, already working.

## Benediction

May the reed be kept wherever it stands, and planted where it has fallen. May engineers count the living edge among their structures. And may the word *permanent* be reserved for things that breathe.
