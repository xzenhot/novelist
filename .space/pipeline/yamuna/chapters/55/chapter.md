# The Old Woman's Blessing

## Question

What does the last generation that knew the river's infancy remember? Somewhere on my bank sits an old woman who saw a cleaner Yamuna in her girlhood — the last living person who drank of me before the wound. What would she bless, and whom, before she goes?

## Oration

She sits where she has sat across a long life, and she remembers a different river. She has told it as a memory of her girlhood: the full clean water where the men of the ghat washed and the women drew, few and clear, where the children played at the water's edge and the fish crowded the steps. She is patient, and she has carried that memory through every change, and her memory is a form of keeping. In her blessing there is almost no anger — because the very old hold the good mostly, and forgive the wrong that men have done, and ask only that the small of the new world should not know the wound whole.

Her blessing is simple: let the water come back clean for the little children; let them see a river that can hold a sky; let them cross a bank with no shame. The oldest witness on my bank does not want revenge; she wants a renewal as a gift to those who came after. I bow, if a river can bow, before that blessing — she is the last bridge to the river that once was, and her memory is the only book in which that old water is still written. Guard her blessing, city, as you guard the bank; when she is gone, her memory will speak only as long as it is remembered.

## Benediction

Let the old woman's blessing be recorded and kept, word for word. Let the memory of the clean river outlast the one who held it. And may the young who one day drink a healed Yamuna recognize that they owe it — probably more than they know — to the old woman who remembered the way.
