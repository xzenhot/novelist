# The Old Woman's Blessing

## Question

What does the last generation that knew the river's infancy remember? Somewhere on my bank sits an old woman who saw a cleaner Yamuna in her girlhood. Her memory is the final living witness of the water that was. What would she bless, and whom, before she goes?

## Oration

She sits where her mother sat, cup in hand, old enough to have drunk me before I was this wound. She remembers the peacocks on the bank, the washing of the dye, the little fish that crowded the steps when she was a girl. She is patient; she remembers me whole, and her memory is a form of keeping. In her blessing she says almost nothing of fury: she says, let the water come back clean for the little children; let them see the egret as she saw it; let them know the cold clear drink. The oldest witness on the bank does not want my revenge; she wants my renewal as a gift to those who came after. I bow to the old woman's blessing, the truest testimony I carry. She is the last bridge to the river that was. Guard her blessing, city, as much as the bank. When she is gone, her memory will speak only as long as she is remembered.

## Benediction

Let the old woman's blessing be recorded and kept, word for word. Let the memory of the clean river outlast the one who held it. And may the young who one day drink a healed Yamuna acknowledge that they owe it to the old woman who remembered the way.
