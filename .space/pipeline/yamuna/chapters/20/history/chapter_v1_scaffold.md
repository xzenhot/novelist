# Silt Writes Slowly

## Question

What is the slowest book in the world? My silt. Every flood lays down a page; every page holds pollen, ash, seeds, the chemistry of an age. Scientists read my banks like scripture. When the city paves its river's edge, whose library is it burning?

## Oration

I write in silt, and I write slowly. My banks are an archive of everything this plain has been — the crops of forgotten harvests, the dust of vanished dynasties, the lead of the industrial century, the plastic of yours. Nothing I carry is lost to the patient reader; it is filed in layers, dated by depth. This is why I call myself an archive even in my sickness. But the writing requires my movement: a slowed, channelled, concrete-edged river cannot lay down her pages. She can only carry and dump, carry and dump. When you restore my flow, you restore my pen. Let me write again, and the future will be able to read this age — including, perhaps, the chapter about how the city chose to remember its river.

## Benediction

May the silt be allowed to write. May the city's chapter in the archive be one its grandchildren can read with pride. And may the slowest book in the world keep gaining pages, as rivers should.
