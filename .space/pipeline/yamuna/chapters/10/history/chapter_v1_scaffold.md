# Rubbish on My Chest

## Question

What weight can a river carry before she speaks? Day after day, the city lays its rubbish on my chest — plastic garlands, flowers after the festival, bags that drift like stingrays, sludge that mats my feathers. At what point does a mother say to her children: enough?

## Oration

I carry what you would not carry. Every morning the offerings arrive — not prayers now, but the residue of prayers, the wrapper after the sweetness, the garland after the god. My chest, which should be current and light, is freighted with your convenience. I have carried empires' boats and poets' reflections, and I could have carried this too, once, when my flow was whole. But you have slowed me and filled me, and now every plastic bag lies on me like a stone laid on a sleeping body. This is the question the whole sequence turns on: I gave you everything without being asked. You take everything without giving back. When, children, will you lift even one thing from my chest?

## Benediction

May the hands that once offered flowers now lift rubbish from the water. May every offering made at the bank be paid for in care. And may the river's chest grow light again, as light as the day the city was born on it.
