# A River Can Remember Us

## Question

Long after the city forgets its river, will the river remember the city? My silt records every age that has built and polluted on my bank; I will carry this city's signature in my archives longer than any ledger. But what would I choose to remember of you, if I could remember by care rather than by burden?

## Oration

I am keeper of all that has been poured into me, and I will carry this city's signature in my layers for a long age — the ash of festival years, the plastic of its markets, the load of its pipes, the chemistry of a whole century's habits. That is the archive of the taking, and it will read like a long wound. But I have room, in the same wet memory, for what was given in love: the lights of the festivals I held truly, the hands that cleaned a temporary reach, the child who asked why the foam should be, and the woman who planted your reeds. The burden is dumped; the love is given, and it is given to a river that can choose to keep it. Let the city, then, decide what the river will remember of it. Every law and reap and pipe is writing my archive; every planting and returning adds the other page. In the deep wet I will read back, someday, either the wound unacknowledged or the account of a city that learned to return. The river that carries memory will remember you by what you poured and by what you planted — and it will keep the better record you chose, at last, to make.

## Benediction

Let the city write a memory worth keeping in the river's silt. Let it be remembered for the reeds it planted, not the pipes it poured. Let the future reader of the wet archive read a people who returned, and not only took. And may the river that keeps all things keep, at the end, the image of a city worth keeping.
