# A River Can Remember Us

## Question

Long after the city forgets its river, will the river remember the city? My silt records every age that has built and polluted on my bank. I will remember this city through its debris in my archive. But what would I choose to remember, if I could remember by love rather than by burden?

## Oration

I am keeper of all that was poured into me, and I will carry this city's signature in my layers for a long time yet — the ash of its fires, the plastic of its market days, the load of its pipes. But I would rather remember the city by other means: the festival lights I held truly, the hands that cleaned a temporary reach, the child who asked why the foam should not be. I can remember the burden or the love; the burden is dumped, but the love is given, and it is given to a river that can choose to keep it. Let the city decide what the river will remember of it. It is writing its own sediment with every choice of pipe and reed. In the deep archive of the wet, I will read back, someday, either a wound unrepented or the account of a city that learned to return.

## Benediction

Let the city write a memory worth keeping in the river's silt. Let it be remembered for the reeds it planted, not the pipes it poured. Let the future reader of the wet archive read a people who returned, not only took. And may the river that keeps all things keep, at the end, the image of a city worth keeping.
