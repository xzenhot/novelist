# The River's Patient Anger

## Question

Does a river have a right to anger? I have been patient as a mother — I have given and given, forgiven and forgiven, absorbed and endured. But a patience that never breaks becomes silence, and a river that accepts the rut and the choke without complaint has already begun to die. When I rise and take back the bank, is it only grief, or is it, at last, an informed anger?

## Oration

They name my floods vengeance, as if the water were cruel. Let me be honest, in the voice of the one most wronged: I am not all gift and no right. A body that is walled and narrowed and filled, that is asked to carry a century's waste and smile in the hymns, learns to speak in the only voice left to it. When I rise and reclaim the ground built on my lung, I am not punishing you; I am righting a geometry — showing the water to the place where the water has always gone, because the concrete denied it anywhere else.

I would rather not have to shout. This anger is the record of a wound pressed too long; it is the water demanding back the room it needs to be a river. Let the city read that anger as the warning it is — not spite, but the arithmetic of a body pushed past its shape. The angry river is the one that still wants to live; the river that goes silent and accepts the rut is the one already fading into a drain. Do not fear my anger. Listen to it. It is the truest speech I have left, and it will end only when the floodplain is given back.

## Benediction

Let the river's anger be read, not feared, as the speech of a long-held hurt. Let it wake the city to its debt and to the geometry it has already broken. And may the anger, once answered with a floodplain and a door, subside at last into the oldest patience — the calm of a river that has been heard.
