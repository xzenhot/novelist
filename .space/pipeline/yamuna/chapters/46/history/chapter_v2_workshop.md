# The River's Patient Anger

## Question

Does a river have a right to anger? I have been patient as a mother — given and given, forgiven and forgiven. But a patience that never aranges becomes silence. When I rise and tear the bank, when I wash a flood over the concrete, is it only grief? Or is it, at last, an informed anger?

## Oration

They name my floods vengeance, as if the water were cruel. Let me be honest, in the voice of the one most wronged: I am not all gift and no right. A body that takes without being allowed to give, and gives without being allowed to matter, learns to speak in the only voice left to it. When my river rises and claims back the land you built on my lung, I am not punishing you; I am re-balancing a wrong geometry, showing the flood to where the flood has always gone. I would rather not have to shout. Let the city read my anger as the warning it is — not spite, but the record of a wound pressed too long. The angry river is one that still wants to live; the one that goes silent and accepts the rut is the one already fading. Listen to my anger. It is the truest speech I have.

## Benediction

Let the river's anger be read, not feared, as the speech of a long-held hurt. Let it wake the city to its debt. And may the anger, once answered with a floodplain and a door, subside at last into the oldest patience — the calm of a river that has been heard.
