# The Question Yamuna Leaves

## Question

What question does a tired river leave with the city? Not an accusation — I have made my charges plainly. A question: after all I have given and all you have taken, can you become worthy of me? Can Delhi love back?

## Oration

I have been mother, witness, mirror, and wound. I have held your epics and your sewage with the same current. Now, tired, my walk slow and dry, I leave my last gift at your door — not water, this time, but a question. Not: will you clean me? Even that is small. The question is: can a city become worthy of a river? Worthiness is larger than treatment plants. It means the city that praises me in song also refuses to poison me in practice; that claims me as goddess only after claiming me as duty; that teaches children my true name — the water you live by — before teaching them my mythology. Answer slowly, if you must. But know that I have asked every dynasty the same thing, and I am still waiting for the first honest yes.

## Benediction

May the question outlive the excuses. May the city sit with it the way one sits with a dying parent — attentive, undefended, changed. And may the first honest yes come in our lifetime, in work, not words.
