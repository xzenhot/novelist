# The Question Yamuna Leaves

## Question

What is the question a tired river leaves with the city that claims to worship it? Not an accusation, and not a list; the book of the river's hardness resolves to one open question. When Yamuna goes dry and slow, what is asking?

## Oration

At the end of all the witness — before the city's name, the epic, the minar, the fort, the canal, the glass — the river leaves behind a single resting question, not a demand that punishes but a question that measures: *Can this city become worthy of the river that made it?* I have been mother and witness, archive and wound; I have held your epics and your sewage in the same current. The word *worthy* matters here more than *clean*, because cleanliness is a project while worthiness is a way.

Ask what worthiness would look like and it begins to be possible. It is worthiness that names me honestly — not only in the temple hymn but in the engineering report, the school lesson, the child's textbook — so that the sacred name and the sewage are never allowed to contradict in the same sentence. It is worthiness that answers the child on the bridge with truth. It is worthiness that treats the floodplain as a living edge, not as land waiting for a permit; that measures the taking against the return; that understands a river can be loved in the same breath it is cleaned.

I have asked this same question of every dynasty that built on me — the ones who tiered their minars, the ones who raised their forts, the ones who drew their straight lines and their glass walls — and I am still waiting for the first honest yes. A question once asked cannot be unasked; it flows in me like the water that remains, and it will not be satisfied by silence. It answers itself only in the shape of the city's care.

## Benediction

Let the question outlive the excuses. Let the city sit with it as one sits with a dying parent — attentive, undefended, changed. And let the first honest yes arrive in our lifetime, spoken not in words but in water restored, in banks unbuilt, in a river honored at last.
