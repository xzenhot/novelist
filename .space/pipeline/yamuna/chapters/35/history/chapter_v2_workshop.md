# The Metro Above the Water

## Question

What does it mean to cross a river without seeing her? The metro leaps my water at speed, hundreds of thousands overhead every day, eyes on phones and hoardings. No river visible, no ghat, no oar. When a river becomes infrastructure the city travels over, does the city still know her?

## Oration

I am proud of the speed above me — it means the city moves, that it has mastered the plain. But mark the geometry of the crossing: the metro is the modern bridge, and unlike the old bridges, it does not pause. There is no railing to lean on, no fare stop at my center, no fishermen waving below. From my surface the passengers are a blur of light on glass, carrying the city's future overhead and never glancing down. I do not ask for the metro to slow; that would be foolish. I ask only for one kind of mass transit to be the exception — a platform, even a small one, where the river is named and the riders are told: *this is Yamuna, who made this city.* Speed that leaves the river unspoken will, in a generation, leave it unremembered.

## Benediction

May the crossing occasionally be punctuated by memory. May one sign, one window, one voice over the loudspeaker name the river. And may the city that travels above the water be taught, at least once, to look below it.
