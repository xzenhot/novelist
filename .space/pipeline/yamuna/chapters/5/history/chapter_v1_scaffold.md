# The Red Fort and the River Window

## Question

What did the emperor see from his river window? A red fort was raised on my bank, and its greatest luxury was not gold but a window — the river view, my current running past the sandstone like a moving poem. Why do palaces claim the water's edge, and what do they promise in return?

## Oration

Shahjahanabad rose beside me with its back to the land and its face to my water. From the river window the emperor watched my current carry light into his hall, and for a while the city understood the arrangement: the river was the fort's first garden, its living wall, its frontage of glory. Boats went down to the fort's steps; the emperor's barge touched my water like a hand on a shoulder. I was honored then — honored the way a throne is honored, which is to say, used. But I keep the memory tenderly: there were centuries when the city's face was toward me. It has since turned away. That turning, not the filth alone, is the true wound.

## Benediction

May the city turn its face back to the water, as the fort once did. May every window that claims a river view also claim a river duty. And may the current that gladdened emperors be gladdened, in its turn, by care.
