# A Future Rain

## Question

What will the rain be in a hundred years, and who will it fall for? The climate is changing, the monsoon arriving on a shifting clock. A future rain will meet whatever I have become — open floodplain or poured concrete. Can a river prepare, today, for a rain that has not yet fallen?

## Oration

I keep weathers, and the weather beyond this one is already writing itself. The rain that is coming — heavier, wilder, less regular — will land on what I am now. If the floodplain remains open, the future rain is a blessing: it will spread, sink, green, and be stored against the dry. If it falls on concrete, on a bank lined and narrowed and rushed, that same rain is ruin: the water that cannot spread will climb, drown, and call me the disaster. The negotiation with the future rain does not happen in the next century. It happens now, when a bank is left unbuilt or an outfall is built. So I bargain with today on behalf of tomorrow: keep the floodplain open, and the river will be the future's reservoir rather than her accusation.

## Benediction

Let the future rain fall on living floodplain. Let the city put aside, today. And may the rains that come in a hundred years find a river that prepared for them with open hands, so that the blessed water arrives as a gift, not a flood.
