# A Future Rain

## Question

What will the rain be in a hundred years, and who will it fall for? The climate is changing; the monsoon is arriving on a shifting clock, and the city's own weather is no longer the weather it once knew. A future rain will fall on whatever I have become — open floodplain or poured concrete. Can a river prepare, today, for a rain that has not yet fallen?

## Oration

I keep weathers, and the weather beyond this one is already writing itself. The rain that is coming — heavier, wilder, less regular — will land on what I am now. If the floodplain remains open, the future rain is a blessing: it spreads, sinks, greens, and is stored against the dry. If it falls on concrete, on a bank that has been lined and narrowed and rushed, that same rain is ruin — the water that cannot spread will climb, drown the street and the underpass, and name me the disaster when I am only the messenger.

The negotiation with the future rain does not happen in the next century. It happens now, when a bank is left unbuilt or an outfall is blocked, when a floodplain is saved or sold. I bargain with today on behalf of tomorrow: keep the floodplain open, and I will be the future's reservoir rather than its accusation. A city is storing its own misfortune when it paves the room the rain will need. Prepare the bank now, and the blessed water of the future arrives as a gift — held, absorbed, welcomed, said over the years.

## Benediction

Let the future rain fall on living floodplain. Let the city put aside, today, the room the coming sky will need. And let the rains that fall a century hence find a river that prepared for them with open hands, so that the blessed water arrives as a gift and not as a flood.
