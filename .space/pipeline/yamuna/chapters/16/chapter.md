# The Floodplain's Green Breath

## Question

What breathes where the city draws its building lines? The floodplain — the low green margin where I spread in the monsoon and rest in the dry months — is labelled "vacant" in the surveys and made ready for plinths. But vacancy is a surveyor's word. What actually lives in that green breath, and what dies when it is filled?

## Oration

The floodplain is my lung, and the city has been reading my lung-reading as an empty lot. In the full river this margin widens; in the lean it greens and rests — and all year it works: the reeds and grasses root my bank, slow the flood, shelter the birds and the small creatures, and filter the runoff before it reaches my current. The surveys call this ground vacant because nothing human stands on it; but the vacancy is the point. It is the room I breathe in when the monsoon brings me high, and the room the city's wells draw from when the ground water is recharged by my spread. Fill the floodplain and you do not gain land; you remove a membrane and open the city to the flood it thought it was planning away.

The engineers who build on me do not deny that the flood comes; they only believe the wall will stop it. But a river that is not allowed to spread will climb, find the street, the underpass, the basement, and reclaim by violence what was taken by permit. I am not threatening; I am describing hydraulics. Keep my green breath open — leave the floodplain as floodplain — and I will absorb the rains, feed the aquifer, shelter the birds, and stay a servant. Pave it, and I will meet you in the streets with the very water you refused to house.

## Benediction

Let the floodplain stay green and unbuilt, as the river's lung. Let the surveys learn the word *breathing* and write it where they once wrote *vacant*. And let the city's memory of floods be honored by keeping the river's room open, not by betting the basement against the monsoon.
