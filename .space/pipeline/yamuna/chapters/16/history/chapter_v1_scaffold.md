# The Floodplain's Green Breath

## Question

What breathes where the city wants to build? My floodplain — the low green edge where I spread in monsoon and rest in the dry months — is called vacant on the surveys. But vacancy is a human word. What actually lives in the floodplain, and what dies when it is filled?

## Oration

The floodplain is my breathing edge. In monsoon I open it like a lung; the water spreads, reeds rise, waders descend, the ground drinks a year's supply for the wells that will be asked for later. This is not idle land. It is the machinery of my health and the city's quiet insurance against flood and thirst. Every construction on it is a lung closed with brick. The engineers who fill it do not deny my breath; they simply never asked for it. And the flood, when it comes, does not negotiate with concrete. I say to the planners with all a mother's plainness: leave me my green breath, and I will absorb your rains, feed your aquifer, shelter your birds. Pave my breath, and you will meet me in your streets.

## Benediction

May the floodplain stay green and unbuilt, as the river's lung. May the surveys learn the word *breathing* and write it where they wrote *vacant*. And may the city's flood memory be honored by keeping the river's room open.
