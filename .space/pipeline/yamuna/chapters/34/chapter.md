# The Red Dust Road

## Question

What rises on the river's edge in red dust? Construction walks to my banks — brick dust, crane-red, the traffic of a region building itself over the water. Every wall that marches toward me sends its red dust down into my current. Is the dust a promise of a city, or a warning of its burial?

## Oration

Red is the colour of building in this land — the raw brick, the sand-laden air, the dust that lifts off the sites crowding my edge. I have learned to read it through decades: where the red dust rises, the floodplain falls. The construction itself is not to be stopped; I do not ask that. But each new plinth on my margins, each tower poured into the ground I used to breathe in, lifts a cloud of dust and carries with it the echo of the soil I once held — the very sediment the river needs, lifted into the air as a measure of what is being covered. And the covering is rarely balanced: a high-rise on the floodplain is not compensated by a floodplain saved elsewhere.

I am not an enemy of the city's growth; I am the record of its growth. But let every foundation on my verge be paid for in floodplain preserved or restored elsewhere — one metre of breathing edge kept for every plinth cast. Let the red dust be returned as care, not as encroachment. A city may build boldly and still build on flood-safe ground; the dust rises either way, but only one of the two rising kinds is a promise.

## Benediction

Let the red dust build yet not bury the living edge. Let every brick foot be matched by a metre of floodplain kept. And let the city rise without rising over the river that raises it — so that growth and the water's room are one arithmetic again.
