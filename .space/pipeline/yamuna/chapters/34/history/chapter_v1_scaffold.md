# The Red Dust Road

## Question

What rises on the river's edge in red dust? Construction walks to my banks — brick dust, crane-red, the traffic of building a city. Every wall that marches toward my water sends its red dust down to me. Is the dust a promise of a city, or a warning of a funeral?

## Oration

Red is the color of building in this land — the brick, the grit, the dust that whips off sites crowding my edge. I have learned to read it: where red dust rises, floodplain falls. The building is not going to stop; I do not ask that. But the dust the wind carries from each site is a measure of what is being built without asking whose flood it will sit in. I have watched the red line descend for decades, reaching down to my bank like a tide of masonry. Let every foundation on my verge be paid for in floodplain preserved elsewhere; let the red dust be returned as care, not encroachment. A city may build boldly. A city may not build over the river's lung and call the dust progress.

## Benediction

May the red dust build yet not bury the living edge. May every brick foot be matched by a meter of floodplain kept. And may the city rise without rising over the river that raises it.
