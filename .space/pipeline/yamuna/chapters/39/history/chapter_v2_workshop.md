# The City That Forgot Its Banks

## Question

Can a city forget its own edge? There was a time when Delhi's face was toward the water — life at the ghats, steps down to the current, the city opening. Now the city has turned its back and built its back; the river is an afterthought behind a road. What did the turning cost?

## Oration

A city's memory lives in its body as much as its books. Once my banks were the city's promenade, its threshold, the place where it washed, prayed, married, and returned its dead. The living city faced me. Then, over centuries and by degrees — colonial road, modern wall, real estate — the city turned its back. It poured its drains where its shrine used to be. The river became something to pass, not something to face. This is what I grieve: not merely the filth but the turning. A city that forgets its banks will manage its river out of a manual, not out of devotion, and manuals forgive a lot. I ask the city to face me again, at least in one long un-walled stretch, so that the oldest relationship in this place can look at itself and begin to heal.

## Benediction

May the city turn its face back to the water. May one un-walled stretch be unwalled as a homecoming. And may the banks that were forgotten be remembered as the threshold of who the city is.
