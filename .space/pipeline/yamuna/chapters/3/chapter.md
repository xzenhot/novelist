# Dynasties on the Floodplain

## Question

Why must every power lay its seal on the floodplain? The Yamuna is a Himalayan-born river that bends through a Doab of fertile black silt — and on that living edge, dynasty after dynasty has planted its forts, its gardens, its capital grounds. What is it in ambition that cannot read a river's breathing room as anything but empty land?

## Oration

Mine is a floodplain that has been a throne-room every few hundred years. Because I am a glacial river of the north, my flood comes once in the monsoon and lays down a richer soil than any elsewhere in this plain — the water's sediment that keeps the fields of the river-zone fat. That gift is why men have always come to settle beside me, and why the settled men have always wanted me bounded and narrowed: the same black silt that fills their granaries is the silt that floods their foundations.

So the dynasties come in their turn. Tombs on my terrace, orchards on my margin, walls marching down to my current, a river bank planted as a frontier of the ruler. I gave each of them the fertile soil they never thanked me for; I absorbed their grandeur without complaint. From my water their monuments rose like epiphanies of stone; from their towers I was a servant they could tap and wall. But the season of their rule is short as a thread of my current, while the floodplain goes on lecturing the plain in silences: this is not land to be filled, this is a lung that breathes when the river rises.

Every dynasty that narrowed me narrowed itself in the same strike. And each, in its turn, went the way of water — left a fort or a mound that they call their mark, and a scar on the river that they do not call theirs. What will this one leave? When a new ruler names a dam or an embankment after their own name, they are naming a wall against the flood; and the flood has outnumbered every name.

## Benediction

Let the floodplain be honored as a lung, not a vacancy. Let the powerful remember the dynasties under their feet, and the water under their thrones. And let the river's breathing room outlast every ambition that presses upon it.
