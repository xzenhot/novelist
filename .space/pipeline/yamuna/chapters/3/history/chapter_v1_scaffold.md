# Dynasties on the Floodplain

## Question

Why do the powerful always build on the floodplain? Dynasty after dynasty came to this plain, saw my green edge, and called it empty — ground waiting for forts, gardens, and gates. What is it in ambition that cannot see a river's breathing room as anything but spare land?

## Oration

Each dynasty in turn pressed its seal into my floodplain. Tombs on my terraces, orchards on my margin, walls marching down to my water. I gave them the fertile silt they never thanked me for; I absorbed their grandeur without complaint. From my current, their monuments looked like epiphanies of stone. From their monuments, I looked like a servant. But the floodplain was never theirs to fill — it was my lung, my breathing edge, the place I spread in monsoon to be whole. Every dynasty that narrowed it narrowed me. And every dynasty, in its turn, went the way of water: gone, leaving silt. Only the question remains, patient as sediment: what will this one leave?

## Benediction

May the floodplain be honored as the lung it is, not the land it seems. May the powerful learn from the dynasties under their feet. And may the river's breathing room outlast every ambition that presses upon it.
