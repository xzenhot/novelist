# The Minar and the Stone Sky

## Question

How high can ambition climb before it forgets the water below? In the far distance of this plain rises the Qutub Minar — a tower of red sandstone and marble, layer upon layer of victory announced in stone. I watch its summit from my current and ask: what does a tower that drinks from the river decide to remember of the river?

## Oration

The Qutub towers over the old city as a statement of conquest that outlasted every conqueror. Its five storeys of fluted red sandstone lean up as a ladder of prayer and triumph; it was begun in the twelfth century by a sultan succeeding, in his own turn, the rulers he displaced, and it was carried, in its way, by masons hauling the very sandstone that my plain and its outlying ridges supply. The columns that flank its courtyard were cut from older temples — and that is the kind of reuse, the taking of another's stone to proclaim a new sky, that the whole tower is built upon.

I do not begrudge the minar its beauty; it is lovely, and it has outlived its builders' intention, leaning a little now as if considering the weight of its own myth. But mark what the tower could not do: it could not hold a single cup of water. Every throne in that century drank from me while looking up at its new sky. The masons paused at my bank, scooped my water, praised the sultan; the dust of all that carving, sooner or later, came down to me, as everything does.

Height is a human measure and a boast against what runs low. The river is the measure that remains: low, wide, indispensable. When the minar says *I rise*, the answer of the water is *I flow* — and flow has carried the history that rising only marks. Let the tall stones be beautiful; let their stele line the sky; but understand that the city's true upthrust has always rested on the low water that none of them thought worth recording.

## Benediction

Let the tall things of this city remember the low water that raised them. Let those who climb the galleries also look down at the silver curve across the plain. And let the river outlast the stone sky, as patience outlasts announcement.
