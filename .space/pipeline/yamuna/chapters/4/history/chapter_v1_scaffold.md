# The Minar and the Stone Sky

## Question

How high can ambition climb before it forgets the water below? A tower rose over this plain — stone sky, victory in masonry — visible for miles. From its galleries, my silver curve was one more conquest in the view. But what does a tower drink from, and where does its dust go?

## Oration

The minar was raised to pierce my sky, and it did — a stone sky, an announcement in masonry that some names would be lifted above all others. I bore the barges that brought its stone. I heard the masons drink my water and praise the sultan. I do not begrudge the tower its beauty; it is lovely, and it has outlived its builder's intention. But mark what the tower could not do: it could not give one cup of water. Every throne in that century drank from me while looking up. And the dust of all that carving, sooner or later, came down to me, as everything does. Height is a human measure. The river is the measure that remains.

## Benediction

May the tall things of this city remember the low water that raised them. May those who look up from the galleries also look down at the silver curve. And may the river outlast the stone sky, as patience outlasts announcement.
