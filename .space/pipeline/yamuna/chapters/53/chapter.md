# No River Is Alone

## Question

Is any river alone? I am one length of a whole system — fed by peaks, joined by streams, spilling into the sea through lands that are not this city's. And a city is likewise no island, drinking from waters beyond its borders. What does the truth that no river runs alone demand of a city that treats its river as a frontier?

## Oration

No river is alone. I am the youngest water of a continental family — my headwaters draw their snow from the Himalaya, my tributaries reach all the way back to those mountains, and my current is bound to the rivers that run beside me and the sea that waits below. When the city drinks, it drinks the remembered rain of a whole drainage; every drop in the glass is marked with the whole basin. And this family is not a border but a bond. When one reach of the city empties filth into me, the stain travels — it does not stay at the line of an administrative boundary. It drains to the next valley, joins by sisters, reaches the sea, and the whole system shares the injury one town has made.

The city treats me as its frontier, but I am no frontier; I am the shared current of the whole plain and the whole range. Its water-law should therefore be a basin-law, warm and whole, remembering that what this province pours travels to the next. No river is alone, and no town is exempt. The covenant is single — from the snow to the sea — and the city that remembers this will treat its neighbor's reach as it treats its own.

## Benediction

Let the city know that no river runs alone. Let its water-law hold the whole basin, and its heart the whole system. And as the same water passes many banks, let it pass one covenant — from the snow to the sea.
