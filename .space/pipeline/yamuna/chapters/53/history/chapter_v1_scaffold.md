# No River Is Alone

## Question

Is any river alone? I am one length of a whole system — fed by peaks, joined by streams, spilling into lowlands. And a city is likewise no island, drinking from waters beyond its borders. What does the truth that no river is alone demand of a city that treats its river as a frontier?

## Oration

No river is alone. I am the youngest water of a continental family — my headwaters draw their snow from peaks far from this city, my tributaries reach back toward the mountains, my current is bound to the rivers that run beside me. When the city drinks, it drinks the remembered rain of a whole drainage; the drop is marked with the whole basin. And this family is not a border but a bond. When this province pollutes me, the stain drains to the next valley or to my sisters and the sea, and the scales seek their balance. No city is a frontier; no river is alone. Let the city's water law be basin-wide and warm, so that he drinks in this valley remembers the mountain, and the sea. The whole system is one covenant, and no town is exempt.

## Benediction

Let the city know that no river runs alone. Let its water-law hold the whole basin, and its heart the whole system. And as the same water passes many banks, let it pass one covenant — from the snow to the sea.
