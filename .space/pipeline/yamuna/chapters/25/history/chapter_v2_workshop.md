# When Birds Leave the Bank

## Question

Why have the birds gone quiet? The painted storks, the cormorants drying their wings, the winter geese that once wrote calligraphy across my shallows — fewer every year. When the birds leave a river, what are they announcing that the water quality reports have not?

## Oration

Birds are my honest critics. They do not read reports; they read fish, insects, reeds, and mud — and they have been giving my reach a failing grade, one absent species at a time. Where the reeds were cut, the nests went. Where the fish died, the divers left. Where the foam lies thick, even the egrets keep their distance. Their departure is not tragedy alone; it is testimony. And their return is the only audit the city should trust: when the small fish come back, the birds will come back behind them, without a single committee. I ask the city to watch the banks the way it watches the stock market. The birds are the index of my health, and they have been falling for years.

## Benediction

May the wings return to the bank, one cleaned reach at a time. May the city learn to read the birds as its most truthful newspaper. And may the winter skies fill again with the calligraphy of geese.
