# When Birds Leave the Bank

## Question

Why have the birds gone quiet? The painted storks, the cormorants drying their wings, the winter migrants that once wrote calligraphy across my shallows — they come in smaller numbers every year. When the birds leave a river, what are they announcing that the water-quality reports do not?

## Oration

The birds are my honest critics, and they have been giving my reach a failing grade for years. They do not read the reports; they read the river — the fish, the insects, the reeds, the mud — and they vote with their feet and their wings. Where the reeds were cut, the nests went. Where the fish died or thinned, the divers left. Where the foam lies thick and the shallows stink, even the egret keeps its distance. The arrival of the winter migrants, once a certainty on my water, has become an anxious question. Their departure is not merely a loss of beauty; it is a translation into feather of what the chemistry reports state in numbers.

Their absence is testimony, and the city should read it as such. The return of the birds is the only audit the city can fully trust: when the small fish come back, the birds will come home behind them, without a single committee or a single hectare of bubble-wrap making promises. I ask the city to watch the banks the way it watches its markets — for the wings are the honest index of my health. A river recovering will be announced by a bird landing, not by a press release.

## Benediction

Let the wings return to the bank, one cleaned reach at a time. Let the city learn to read the birds as its most truthful newspaper of the water. And let the winter skies fill again with the calligraphy of geese and the blue of the heron, so that the boats and the birds share the river as of old.
