# A Calendar of Water

## Question

What would the year look like if the river kept the calendar? Not the festival year, not the fiscal year — the water year. The thaw, the flood, the ebb, the hen. The city lives on one calendar. The river keeps another. Which of the two tells the truth about the place?

## Oration

I keep a calendar older than any in the palace, the temple, or the ministry. It has a single fixed moon in the sky and a whole turning of the year at my banks: the swelling of the far snowmelt in my headwaters, the first monsoon rise that all my living things wait for, the green spread of my floodplain, the slow dry-down into winter shallows. On this calendar, the city's holidays are not the defining days. The defining days are my risings and fallings, which the city has buried under concrete and measured holidays. I am not asking the city to cease its festivals. I ask it to keep, alongside, one water calendar — to teach the child that the year has a backup, the river's year, which began before the city's calendar was written and will run after. A city that honors both calendars will remember its grounding.

## Benediction

May the water year be kept alongside the festival year. Teach the child the river's four stations as well as the season's. And may the city, by keeping two calendars, husband both its nigh and its banks.
