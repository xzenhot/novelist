# The School Beside the Floodplain

## Question

What do the children learn in the school that stands at the river's edge? Their playground is the floodplain's margin; their textbook carries a picture of me as I was years ago. What education does a river give — and what does the school teach, instead, about the water that made this land?

## Oration

The school came to stand at the edge of my floodplain, and the children who run there do not know whose bank they run on. Their geography shows a clean blue river out of an old photograph; their history a river that is contested; their civic a list of duties with few waters in it. The floodplain just past the school wall is a richer teacher: it floods green in its season, it lays down mud, it brings the wader and the reedbed and the spawning, it idles and it rises. But no bell rings for that lesson.

What education does a river give? It gives the living ground truth of how a land is made — the silt, the flood, the soil that grows the food. And it gives a second truth, of a wound: what a people do to the water they live by. The school keeps both truths just past its own wall and sends the children home past the polluted current without once unpacking what they pass.

I ask the school to step a generation deeper. Teach the floodplain as a subject, not a site; teach the children to read the mud, the birds, the reed, and the drain the way they read a chapter. A child who understands the floodplain by heart will not sell it when they come to power. The school that teaches the water is building the city's whole future conscience. Let the floodplain into the classroom.

## Benediction

Let the school and the floodplain teach each other. Let the child name the egret and the wader, not merely color the river. And let the next generation that runs on this bank know, and protect by heart, the water's room that the present is still learning to value.
