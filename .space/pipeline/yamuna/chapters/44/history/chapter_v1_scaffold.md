# The School Beside the Floodplain

## Question

What do the children learn, in the school that stands at my floodplain's edge? Their textbooks map me from an old photograph, when I was cleaner than I am, and call this living ground "land." What does a river teach, and what does the school leave out?

## Oration

The school came to my edge, and its pupils do not know their own floodplain. They draw me from a map a generation out of date, blue ink on white, a river that never floods and never rests. Outside the same window, the true river rises in the wet and greens in the dry; their paper geography and the newspaper's floodplain are two different places that share an address. I want the school to open its lesson once a floodplain as a subject, not a site. When the egret lands on the wet margin, let them name it; when the dry and foam comes, let them ask why. A child who has seen a floodplain breathing once will hesitate, as an adult, to sell it to a builder. Education and a living river are the same curriculum.

## Benediction

May the school and the floodplain teach each other. Let the child name the egret, not merely locate the river. And may the one lesson every citizen carries from childhood be that the blessed land is a river's room, and a lesson worth keeping.
