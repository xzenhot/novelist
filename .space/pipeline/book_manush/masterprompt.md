# Master Prompt — মানুষ: কেন মানুষ মহান

## Book Identity

- **Book name:** MANUSH
- **Long title:** মানুষ: কেন মানুষ মহান
- **Genre:** Philosophical / Spiritual / Baul-Fakir Literature
- **Language:** Bengali (literary, image-rich, philosophical register)
- **Era:** Timeless / Bengal
- **Core question:** Why is man great?

## The Central Premise

This is a frame-story novel that asks the oldest question of the Baul, the Fakir, the Udasi, the Vairagi, and the home-leaver: **what makes a human being great?**

The answer is never stated directly. It is revealed through journey and image:

- Not wealth, not caste, not name, not power.
- But the inward journey of leaving the false house and finding the person of the heart.
- Man is great because he can walk without owning the path, practice without claiming the fruit, and love without chaining the beloved.

## The Frame

Each chapter is a **workshop** — a modern gathering of seekers, artists, and skeptics who meet to hear a narrated story. The frame characters debate, question, and are changed by each story. The narrator bridges the modern frame and the spiritual narrative.

## The Spiritual Figures (from the Gosai tradition)

Each chapter's narrated story centers on one figure:

1. **Baul** — the wind-touched wanderer; divine restlessness that breaks social chains.
2. **Fakir** — the poor one; poverty that releases attachment and lightens the heart.
3. **Udasi** — the unattached one; walks the road without claiming it.
4. **Vairagi** — the one turned away from worldly attachment; wishes to wake from the dream.
5. **Home-leaver** — the traveler who does not mistake shelter for truth; the true departure is inward.
6. **The Road** — discipline, uncertainty, dust, weather, and song; the path itself is the end.
7. **The Song of the Road** — sung by those who walk; lives in the act of singing.
8. **The Person of the Heart** — the hidden self, the beloved truth within each human being.

All figures gather into one: the **Gosai** — the traveler who walks without owning the path.

## Style Mandate

- **Serious, image-rich, philosophical Bengali prose.**
- **Preserve the spiritual function**, not the phrasing — the language should feel like a song overheard on the riverbank.
- **Show, don't tell.** Reveal greatness through journey, image, and inner turn — never through direct statement.
- **Root imagery in Bengal:** the Padma, the road, the dust, the wind, the open sky, the boat, the far shore.
- **End each chapter with a running summary** that connects to the next chapter and sustains curiosity.

## The Three-Section Structure

Every chapter preserves three sections:

1. **Workshop (frame)** — the modern scene where characters discuss. Keep unchanged.
2. **Story (narrative)** — the narrated spiritual story. Rewrite richly, in batches, to the target length.
3. **Discussion (frame)** — the characters' reactions. Keep unchanged.
