Gibran's prophetic voice (biblical, aphoristic, oracular) is the natural carrier for this spiritual material, and the ten Stoic themes map onto the Buddha's journey.

## Theme Assignments

Each chapter is assigned one Stoic theme (from `generic.md`), mapped onto a present-day concern.

| Chapter | Name | Theme | Contemporary Mapping |
|---------|------|-------|----------------------|
| 1 | The Sheltered Prince | The Last Change | The modern denial of aging, sickness, and death — the four sights we scroll past every day. |
| 2 | The Great Renunciation | The Uncluttered Soul | The modern burden of possessions and status — the freedom found in letting go. |
| 3 | The Awakening | The Woven Whole | The modern sense of a separate, isolated self — the realization that the self is not separate from the whole. |
| 4 | The Teaching | The Royal Service | The modern question of meaning — finding nobility in compassion and service rather than status. |
| 5 | The Echo | The Fading Name | The modern fear of being forgotten — the truth that what endures is the teaching, not the name. |

## Weaving Notes

- **Embody, don't explain** — each theme is carried through image and cadence, not stated directly.
- **Return to the metaphor family** — each chapter draws on its theme's imagery (e.g. "the seed" for The Last Change, "the empty vessel" for The Uncluttered Soul).
- **Honor the signature** — Gibran's three-part form (the question, the answer, the benediction) carries each theme.
- **Ground in the epic** — every theme is native to the epic's threads (the sheltered life, the renunciation, the awakening, the teaching, the echo).
