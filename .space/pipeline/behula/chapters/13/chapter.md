# The Blood She Gave

There came a day when the doctors said he needed blood, and the blood was not there. The banks were empty, the donors were gone, the city had given all it had and still it was not enough. And Behula, who had given everything else, understood that there was one thing left to give, and that it was the thing that ran in her own veins.

She rolled up her sleeve without being asked, and she offered what no one had thought to ask of her. The needle found the vein, and the blood left her body, and she watched it go with a strange and quiet pride, because it was the first thing she had been able to give that no one could take from her. It was hers, and she gave it freely, and in the giving of it she understood what devotion truly is: not the grand sacrifice, but the small, bodily, unglamorous gift of the self.

The blood was tested, and matched, and carried away to the man who lay behind the glass, and Behula sat in the corridor and felt the faint dizziness of what she had given, and she did not regret it. She had given her blood, and her blood would go into him, and for a little while they would share the same current, the way they had once shared a life. And that, she thought, was a kind of marriage that no sickness could dissolve.

She gave again, when they asked, and again, and each time she gave she felt herself grow lighter and stronger at once, as if the emptying of her body was also a filling of her soul. The world had taken so much from her, but this it could not take, because this she gave. And in the giving, she discovered a truth that the world had tried to hide from her: that a woman who has nothing can still be a giver, and that the poorest heart can be the richest in love.

For blood is the river that runs inside us, the hidden water that carries life from one body to another. And Behula, who had once carried her love across a river of death, now carried it in her own veins, and gave it, drop by drop, to the man she would not let die. And the blood she gave was not a sacrifice. It was a promise, written in the only ink that never fades.
