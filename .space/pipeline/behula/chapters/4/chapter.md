# The River's Road

The city was not kind to her, and she did not ask it to be. It rose against her in queues that stretched around the hospital and turned her away at the gate; it fell away in locked pharmacies and empty shelves where the medicine she needed was gone; it carried her past strangers who would not meet her eyes, and past nights when the fear came up out of the dark and wrapped itself around her like a second shroud. And through all of it she spoke to the city as one speaks to a road that is also an enemy, and also a friend.

She learned that the hospital is the threshold between the living and the dying, and that to walk it is to belong to neither side. The living looked at her from behind their masks and saw a ghost; the dying lay behind the glass and did not answer. And so she became a creature of the between, a woman who had left the world of the well but had not yet been received by the world of the sick, and who carried her love through the middle of both.

Hunger hollowed her. The sun burned her. The rain fell on her and on the papers she guarded alike, and she could not always tell which of them was the more fragile. But she did not turn back, because turning back would have meant returning to the silence she had refused, and the silence was worse than any queue. The city could break her body, but it could not break the vow she had made in the dark of that first night.

And so she went on, day after day, a woman and a sick man and a few papers, moving through the long streets toward a doctor she had never met and could not be sure would help. She had no map but her love, and no compass but her fear, and no companion but the city that carried her and fought her in the same current.

And in the long loneliness of that road, she began to understand that the city was not only a road. It was a test. It was the water that separates the woman who loves from the woman who merely mourns, and it would not let her pass until she had proven which one she was.
