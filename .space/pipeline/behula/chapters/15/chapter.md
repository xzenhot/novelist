# The Morning He Breathed

And then, on a morning that looked like every other morning, the door opened, and the doctor came out, and his face was not the face of a man bringing bad news. He said the words that Behula had stopped daring to hope for, and the words were simple, and they were everything: he is breathing on his own. The machine had done its work, and the lungs had begun to remember, and the man who had been carried to the edge of the dark had turned, and was coming back.

Behula did not cry out, and she did not fall. She stood very still, the way she had stood on the first night, and she let the words settle into her like rain into dry earth. She had carried so much for so long that she had forgotten what it felt like to be light, and now the lightness came, and it was almost too much to bear. The vigil was over. The road was ending. The love that had refused to let go had been answered.

She was allowed to see him, at last, through the glass and then, later, beside him, and she looked at the face she had loved and fought for and bled for, and she saw that it was still there, still his, still breathing. The sickness had taken so much, but it had not taken him, and it had not taken her, and it had not taken the love that had carried them both through the long dark.

And in that moment she understood that she had not saved him alone. The doctors had saved him, and the medicine had saved him, and the blood had saved him, and the city, in its grudging way, had saved him. But she had been the one who would not let go, and that, she knew, had been the first and necessary thing. For the body can be healed by medicine, but the will to live is healed by love, and she had loved him back from the edge of the dark.

And so she sat beside him, and she held his hand, and she did not speak, because there were no words for what she felt. She only held on, the way she had held on through every day of the long road, and she understood that this was the benediction she had been walking toward all along: not a triumph, but a return. The morning he breathed was the morning she began, at last, to breathe again too.
