# The Mockery of the World

They stood in the queues and they stared at her, and their stares followed her through the streets like a flock of birds that would not scatter. Look at the housewife, they said, out alone in the sickness, chasing a dying man through the city. Look at the woman who will not accept what the doctors have written. And they threw their looks at her as if looks could sink a raft, as if a man's stare were a stone heavy enough to drag her under.

But Behula did not answer them with anger, for she had learned that anger is a weight, and her raft was already heavy enough. She answered them with the only thing she had left: the steady pull of her errands, the unbroken line of her course, the quiet certainty of a woman who has already chosen her road and will not be turned from it. And in that silence she was speaking louder than any of them.

For she understood that the world measures love by what it can see, and what it can see is very little. It can see a housewife and a sick man and a few papers, and it calls the whole thing madness. But it cannot see the vow that was made in the dark, or the fortress that was built in the heart, or the road that runs beneath the streets where no eye can follow. And because it cannot see these things, it mocks them, the way the blind mock the sighted for walking toward a light they cannot perceive.

Let them stare, she thought. Let them call me mad. For the love that carries me is not a thing that can be weighed on their scales or counted in their ledgers. It is not measured by what the world can see; it is measured by what the heart can bear. And the heart can bear more than the world knows.

And so she passed them, queue after queue, and their stares grew fainter behind her, until at last it was only the sound of the streets and the sound of her own breathing, and the long silence of a woman who had stopped listening to the world and begun listening to something deeper. For the world's mockery is a small thing, and it passes. But a woman's devotion is a great thing, and it remains.
