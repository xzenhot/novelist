# The Volcanic Fire

All evening I had felt the heat within the pavement rising through my shoes. By the time I reached my room in Dwarka, I wanted to call it anger, to give the burning a human purpose and a human enemy. I opened the window, but the air outside was scarcely cooler. Below me the city shone as though it had discovered how to keep the sun after sunset. I laid my palm upon the sill and listened to the traffic moving through the warmth.

I am Sagorika, and the epic calls the volcanic fire my origin. In the country of its images, that fire beats beneath every stone. I know the danger of speaking a metaphor until it hardens into an explanation: a poem cannot date the rock by the fierceness of its longing. Tonight I did not ask the ground to prove my vision. I let the inward flame show me the desire with which I had entered the room, the wish to build something so bright that no darkness could question me.

Upon the table lay a page I had written that morning. I had filled it with towers, with the triumph of the city, with a woman whose endurance made every injury seem small. Reading it now, I heard the cruelty hidden in her invulnerability. If she could survive anything, why should anyone cease to wound her? I struck out one sentence. Beneath it another made the same boast in a gentler voice. Soon the page resembled a field after hard weather, its dark lines broken by darker crossings, its beautiful certainty no longer fit to display.

I was tempted to tear it into pieces. Destruction offered a purity revision could not promise. The hand which had raised the false city could clear it in an instant and congratulate itself upon beginning again. Instead I turned the page and wrote one ordinary thing: my feet hurt. Then another: the air was hot, and I had left the water standing far from where I sat. I rose to bring it nearer. The cup touched my mouth with a small coolness which required no empire to explain it. From that modest beginning the woman upon the page became capable of asking for care.

The night did not cool at my command. I worked beside the open window, keeping what remained truthful, letting the rest return to unwritten possibility. My fire had become the patience to stay with a damaged sentence until it could shelter a breathing life. Below, a window went dark; farther away another opened. I could neither bless nor condemn the whole city from my chair. I could refuse to make its endurance an alibi. May the flame I carry give enough light for that refusal, and enough warmth for the labour after it. I set the unfinished page beside the cup, where its corner would remember the faint wet circle of something received rather than conquered.
