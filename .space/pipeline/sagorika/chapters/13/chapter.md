# The Red Lipstick

The colour would not settle evenly upon my lips. I leaned closer to the mirror, wiped one edge, and tried again. Outside my room in Dwarka the evening was assembling its bright invitations, and I had promised to meet someone before the shops closed. I could hear his earlier praise returning to me: you look as though nothing has ever hurt you. He had meant kindness. Yet the compliment had remained beneath my skin like a demand I had not agreed to obey.

I am the woman in red, but red is not a contract with another person's gaze. I turned the small cylinder in my hand and watched the colour rise from its case. The epic made it the memory of fire laid over ancient stone, the city's newest mask upon its oldest face. In the mirror I saw a more intimate difficulty. If I covered the tiredness at the corners of my mouth, would I also be consenting to keep silent about the day? If I left it visible, must truth always arrive without adornment, apologizing for its desire to be beautiful?

A message appeared upon the phone beside the basin. He was waiting already. I began to type an apology, then erased it. The room seemed suddenly crowded with all the versions of myself I had prepared for being received: the cheerful one, the tireless one, the one whose long memory served only to deepen her mystery. Beneath them stood the woman who wanted to sit for a little while. I wrote that I needed more time. The sentence had no ornament. I watched it leave me with more apprehension than any confession about vanished rivers would have caused.

While I waited, I cleaned the red mark from the basin. A little water loosened its edge and carried it across the white surface. I thought of the city polishing its towers, of every bright account which leaves the labour outside the frame. Beauty need not be accused for the concealment practised in its name. The fault began when a surface was permitted to answer questions that only a living voice could answer. I could wear the colour and still speak from beneath it; the two acts need not become rivals for my face.

His answer came while the cloth was still wet. He would wait, he said, and we could sit somewhere quiet. I did not make this small courtesy the ending of a great injustice. It was simply a little room opened inside the evening, and I entered it. I returned to the mirror and finished the uneven edge without hurrying. The red remained a pleasure I had chosen. May the city grant its own brightness such honesty, allowing what shines to disclose a life rather than silence it. When I left, I carried no promise of invulnerability. I carried the colour upon my mouth and the right to use that mouth for whatever truth the night required of me.
