# The Mughals

## Question

What is the beauty of a civilization that believes itself eternal? The Mughals built gardens and forts and cities of such grace that the world still speaks of them, and they believed their empire would never end. Does beauty make a civilization more lasting, or only more mourned when it falls? And what does the earth feel when the most beautiful of its children fades?

## Oration

I remember the Mughals, who built on this plain as if they were planting a garden that would bloom forever. They raised the Red Fort and the great mosques and the walled city of Shahjahanabad, and they filled them with water and marble and the sound of fountains, and for a time it seemed that beauty itself had taken a throne. They believed, as every dynasty believes, that their empire was the last word, that the world would never again be without them.

I held their gardens the way I hold all gardens, and I loved them the way the earth loves whatever grows on it, without asking how long it will last. But I knew, as I have always known, that even the most beautiful thing is borrowed, that the marble would weather and the fountains would fall silent and the empire would pass, as every empire passes, into the memory of the ground.

The Mughals are gone now, and their beauty remains only in stone and in the stories the city tells itself. But the earth does not mourn them, because the earth knows that beauty is not lost when it fades; it is only returned. The garden returns to the soil, the marble returns to the mountain, and the civilization that believed itself eternal returns to the ground that was always its true home.

## Benediction

May the city build with beauty, and also with the knowledge that beauty is borrowed. May it love its gardens without believing they are forever. And may the earth, which has held every beautiful thing and let it go, still teach the city that the only eternity is the ground itself.
