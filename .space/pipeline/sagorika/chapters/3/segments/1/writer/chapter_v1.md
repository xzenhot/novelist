# The Vedic Age

## Question

What does a civilization owe to its first prayers? When the hymns were first sung and the fire altars first lit on this plain, when the words that would become a people's soul were first spoken into the smoke — did those who sang them know they were laying down a debt that every generation after them would carry? And have we, who live in the city they never imagined, kept faith with that first fire?

## Oration

I remember the Vedic age, when the plain was young and the rivers ran clear and the people gathered around fire to speak to the sky. I was the earth beneath their altars, and I felt the heat of their first offerings rise through me. They sang of the dawn and the waters and the fire that carries the word upward, and their voices were the first music this land ever heard. They did not build cities then; they built a way of seeing, a way of naming the world, and that naming has never left me.

But the names they sang have faded, and this is the sorrow of every age: that the words which once held a people together become, in time, only words. The Saraswati ran through that age like a bright thread, and they praised her as the mother of rivers, the giver of speech and wisdom. They did not know that she would one day vanish into the sand, that the river they called eternal would become a memory, that the hymns they sang would one day be sung by no one, or sung by rote, without the fire that first gave them breath.

And yet the debt remains, and a debt is not cancelled by forgetting. The fire has become the neon of Dwarka, and the hymns have become the noise of traffic, and the people who once gathered around the altar now gather around their screens. But the earth still listens for the voice that first called her sacred, and the fire still waits for the offering that first gave it meaning, and the river, though vanished, still remembers the name by which she was first loved. A civilization that forgets its first prayers has not escaped them; it has only buried them, and the buried thing always returns, asking to be remembered.

## Benediction

May the city remember the fire that came before the neon. May it keep faith with the first words, the first waters, the first offering. And may the earth, who heard the first hymn and has never forgotten it, still find in us a people worthy of the prayer that began us.
