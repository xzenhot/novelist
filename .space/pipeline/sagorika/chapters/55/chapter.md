# The Qutub's Shadow

Her shadow reached the Qutub's shadow before she did. Sagorika noticed the meeting while her companion was looking upward, his mouth parted in the familiar astonishment of height. He asked her to stand still. The tower would rise from behind her shoulder in his picture, lending her its impossible stature. She obeyed for a moment, then watched the dark edge on the ground and wondered which of them was borrowing the other: the monument her living face, or the woman a permanence she could never wear.

Beyond the camera's frame, a man shifted his bag from one shoulder to the other and searched for shade. Sagorika stepped out of the pose to make room beside her. The man thanked her without knowing he had interrupted an arrangement of woman and empire. He set down the bag. Its weight had left a deep crease in his shirt, a brief inscription more legible to her than those above.

She looked upward again. The carved surface exceeded what she could read. Rather than invent the words it held, she let that limit remain between them. A tower could carry faith, ambition, labour, conquest, and the later desires of those who visited it; no single glance untied those strands. Within her the city's old masks stirred, asking to be recognized without being mistaken for the whole face. She felt the ridge beneath the monument, the ground below the upward claim.

Her companion came into the shade. He showed her the interrupted picture: her body half turned, an empty place where he had expected symmetry. She liked it more than he did. It contained the moment she had seen somebody outside the composition. He enlarged the image until her face filled the screen, then understood that this removed the very thing she valued. Slowly he drew his fingers together, returning her to the space around her.

While they watched, the edge of shadow moved. The man and his bag became exposed to the sun again. He rose with a sigh. Sagorika offered to shift farther along, but the shade there was narrow too. They rearranged themselves, a temporary congregation without shared purpose beyond a little relief. The height that had summoned awe now mattered by the brief coolness it gave, and even that gift could not remain where they wanted it.

This was no verdict against the tower. She felt its beauty without requiring beauty to acquit every history gathered around it. The difficulty belonged also to her red mouth, her careful dress, the modern city's luminous claims. To be seen was not always to be understood; to cast a shadow was not always to shelter. She stood in the distinction until it became less like an idea and more like the warmth returning to her feet.

At last they left. Her companion kept the wider photograph. In it the tower rose beyond the frame, but the figures below had room to move. Sagorika looked once more at the ground where their shadows had met. It was empty now, and the afternoon continued.
