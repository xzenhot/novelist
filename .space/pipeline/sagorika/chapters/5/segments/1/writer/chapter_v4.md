# Indraprastha

At the Purana Qila mounds, where every layer of earth tells a different epic and the archaeologists dig for a city that may never have been there, Indraprastha still waits under the grass. The Pandavas came with their fire and their purpose, and they cleared the forest and levelled the ground and laid the first stones of a city that would be remembered for three thousand years. They called it the city of Indra, the king of the gods, and they believed they were building something that would last forever.

And the earth did not resist them, for the earth does not resist what is built upon it; it bears it, the way a mother bears the weight of the child who will one day leave her. It held their palace the way it has held every palace, without complaint. But it remembered the forest, and it remembered the animals that had lived there, and it remembered the water that had run through it, and it knew that a city is not only what it builds but what it destroys to build. The dharma they spoke of was a dharma of kings and wars and dice, and it did not always include the earth that bore them.

And this is the beloved necessity of every city: that it must build, and that in building it must wound the ground that makes it possible. The city rose, and it was beautiful, and it fell, and it rose again under other names and other kings. But the question the earth asked on the day the first tree fell has never been answered, and it is asked again with every tower that rises and every forest that falls. Every dynasty that has built on this plain has built on the same unasked question: what does a city owe to the ground that makes it possible? And the ground, patient and unbroken, is still waiting for the answer, still holding the myth-city that no one can prove and no one can forget.
