# Indraprastha

## Question

Can a city founded in dharma survive the adharma it does to its own earth? When the Pandavas cleared the forest and built Indraprastha on this plain, when they raised their palace and called it the city of the gods, did they ask what the ground beneath them was owed? And does every city since then carry the same unasked question?

## Oration

I was the earth when Indraprastha was founded, and I felt the first axe fall on the forest that had grown on me for ages. The Pandavas came with their fire and their purpose, and they cleared the trees and levelled the ground and laid the first stones of a city that would be remembered for three thousand years. They called it the city of Indra, the king of the gods, and they believed they were building something that would last forever.

And I did not resist them, for the earth does not resist what is built upon it; it bears it, the way a mother bears the weight of the child who will one day leave her. I held their palace the way I have held every palace, without complaint. But I remembered the forest, and I remembered the animals that had lived there, and I remembered the water that had run through it, and I knew that a city is not only what it builds but what it destroys to build. The dharma they spoke of was a dharma of kings and wars and dice, and it did not always include the earth that bore them.

And this is the beloved necessity of every city: that it must build, and that in building it must wound the ground that makes it possible. The city rose, and it was beautiful, and it fell, and it rose again under other names and other kings. But the question I asked on the day the first tree fell has never been answered, and it is asked again with every tower that rises and every forest that falls. Every dynasty that has built on this plain has built on the same unasked question: what does a city owe to the ground that makes it possible? And the ground, patient and unbroken, is still waiting for the answer.

## Benediction

May the city remember the forest that was cleared to build it. May it ask, before it raises another tower, what the earth is owed. And may the ground beneath Indraprastha, patient and unbroken, still hold the city that has never once asked her forgiveness.
