# The Tropical Edge

Who chose this meeting place before the conquerors chose its name? Sagorika unfolded a map at the edge of the Ridge, where the road sloped toward the plain. Her companion had drawn their day's journey as a confident line. They would cross the city, reach the river, and return before the heat became severe. Upon paper the hours lay obediently beside the distance. Already the morning air was loosening the neat arrangement.

A gust lifted one corner. He held it down with a stone, then complained that the map omitted the steepness beneath their feet. Sagorika looked beyond the ink toward the softened breadth of the plain. Here was the older arrangement: raised rock, low ground, water's passage, the recurring demand of heat and rain. Roads entered it; governments entered it; neither had been present when the terms were first laid down.

They began walking. In the open stretch he quickened his pace, eager to recover the minutes lost upon the Ridge. Soon he stopped beneath a scant patch of shade and drank more than he had intended. Sagorika shared the remainder of her water. The line on the map could not become thirsty, but the men and women who followed lines had bodies. Every claimed dominion began again with the mouth, the sole, the need to rest.

In her inward vision the dynasties approached this same dependence wearing different splendours. Ridge for defence, river for water, plain for grain: the choices sounded imperial until hunger disclosed their source. The land had offered conditions, not a coronation. She knew the temptation to call such conditions fate and excuse every later cruelty beneath that heavy word. Geography could invite a settlement; it did not command a ruler to close his gate against the thirsty.

At a crossing they found their planned path fenced away. Her companion wished to force a shortcut across the rough ground. Sagorika pointed to a woman carrying a child along the longer shaded side. They followed her direction and discovered a place to refill their bottle. The journey altered. It would end at the river later than intended, and the return would require another means. His irritation lingered, then softened as the child ahead began to sing.

By the water he refolded the map along a crease it had not possessed that morning. Sagorika watched the gesture with a tenderness that surprised her. A plan need not be burned because the world corrected it. It might carry the correction home. She touched the damp neck of their bottle and felt how much of their grand passage had depended upon that small coolness. The city rose behind them, immense in its own estimation. Before it lay the river; beneath it waited the rock. Between those presences a human life could choose arrogance or attention, though neither could choose to need nothing. They stood until the breeze changed, and then made room upon the bank for another weary pair.
