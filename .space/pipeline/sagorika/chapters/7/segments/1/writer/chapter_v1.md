# The Das Dynasty

## Question

What does the earth feel when a new dynasty arrives with banners and stone? When the Das dynasty, the first of the sultans, came to this plain and began to build, did the ground beneath them care what name they carried? And is the earth's indifference to the names of rulers a mercy, or a judgment?

## Oration

I remember the coming of the Das dynasty, the first sultans of Delhi, who arrived with banners and horses and the will to build. They found a city that had been Hindu and made it their own, raising mosques and walls and towers where temples and palaces had stood. They believed they were beginning something, and in a way they were, but they did not know that they were only the first of many, that the plain would see dynasty after dynasty rise and fall and rise again.

And I held their stones the way I have held every stone, and I did not ask what god they prayed to or what language they spoke. For the earth does not read the names carved on its surface; it feels only the weight, and the weight of one dynasty is the same as the weight of another. And this is the royal service that the earth performs for every ruler, the service that no king ever notices: to bear the weight of his ambition, to hold up his walls and his towers and his pride, to be the lowest stone in the arch of his empire, the stone that bears everything and is praised by no one. The Das rulers built their city and ruled it and passed away, and the city remained, and the next dynasty came, and the next, and I held them all with the same patient indifference.

There is a lesson in this that the city has never learned: that the ground does not belong to any ruler, that it outlasts every name, that the only thing that endures is the earth itself and the water that runs through it. The Das dynasty is gone, but the plain that held it is still here, still waiting, still indifferent to whatever name comes next.

## Benediction

May the city learn the earth's indifference, and find in it not coldness but peace. May it know that no name is forever, and that the ground beneath every dynasty is the same ground. And may the plain, which has held so many rulers and remembered so few, still be here when the last banner has fallen.
