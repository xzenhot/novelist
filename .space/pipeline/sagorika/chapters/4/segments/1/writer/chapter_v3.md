# The Vanished Saraswati

Beneath the grid roads of Dwarka, far under the metro tunnels and the parking lots and the foundations of the glass towers, the Saraswati still runs, hidden and patient, the way a vein runs under skin that has forgotten it. She was not a small river. She was the great one, the bright one, the river the poets praised above all others, and she ran through this land like a promise. The people built their first settlements on her banks and drank from her and called her sacred, and for a long time she gave and gave and never asked for anything in return.

Then the land changed, and the river that had been the life of the land began to leave it. The rivers that fed her shifted their course, and the water that had been her life began to sink away into the sand. She did not die all at once. She grew thin, and then thinner, and then she was gone, leaving only a dry bed and a name that the poets kept singing long after the water had left. The people who had praised her as eternal learned that even a river can become a memory.

But the earth has not forgotten her, and this is the truth the city does not know: that the past is not gone, that the vanished river is not vanished, that what the city calls memory is still alive beneath its feet. She is there, in the groundwater that runs under every street, in the wells that remember her taste. She is the water that is remembered but no longer seen, and her absence is a wound that the city carries without knowing it. Every time Delhi thirsts, it is thirsting for her. Every time the city draws water from the ground, it is drawing on the memory of the river it lost, and it does not know it, and it does not thank her, and it does not ask whether the water will one day run out the way she ran out. The city drinks, and forgets, and drinks again. And the river, hidden and patient, keeps running beneath it all, waiting to be remembered.
