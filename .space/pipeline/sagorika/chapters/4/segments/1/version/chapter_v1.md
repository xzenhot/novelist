# The Vanished Saraswati

Sagorika drew a river upon the dust gathered at her window. Her finger made a winding line, then stopped where the sill met the wall. Outside, Dwarka shone with windows that seemed to possess their own stars. I asked her where the Saraswati had gone. She looked at the mark as though it were a letter from someone whose address had vanished. Must every absence, she asked, become a secret presence before you will allow yourself to grieve?

I had wanted the comfort of water continuing unseen, a river beneath the city keeping safe everything the city had lost. The story offered such a lovely darkness that I could almost hear it running. But Sagorika would not make a map from longing. The vanished river belonged to her remembered world, to the hymns and the bright thread of the epic; its name could cross her speech without proving the course of any water under our feet. Her finger rested upon the sill, gathering dust beneath its nail. She drew no tunnel beneath the road, promised no hidden abundance, and left the end of the line unfinished.

From the kitchen came the sound of a vessel filling. We had left it beneath a narrow stream while we talked. The sound changed, rising towards a brighter note, and then water struck the metal rim and spilled. I turned too late to prevent the first overflow. It spread over the counter and fell upon my sleeve. The river I could not find had occupied my whole imagination; the water before me had received none of it. Sagorika reached past me and closed the tap. The abrupt quiet seemed larger than the noise had been.

We gathered the spilled water with a cloth. There was no punishment in her face, and that made it harder to flee into apology. She wrung the cloth over a bowl and set it aside for cleaning. I held the vessel while she wiped its base. The work took only a few minutes, but within those minutes the shape of my question altered. I could mourn a river without making grief an excuse to neglect what remained. I could honour a name without requiring the earth to fulfil the promise I had attached to it.

When we returned to the window, a movement of air had softened the drawing. Its edges no longer separated cleanly from the dust. I lifted my finger to restore them, then let the hand fall. Sagorika opened the window wider. Somewhere below, another household was filling another vessel; a voice called, and was answered. These were the waters of the hour entrusted to us, neither eternal nor imaginary. We stood listening until the sound ceased. May the vanished river keep her name without being forced to carry our denial. And may the visible water find us awake beside its brief abundance, ready to turn the tap, to lift the vessel, to answer the thirst that has arrived while we were speaking of the dead.
