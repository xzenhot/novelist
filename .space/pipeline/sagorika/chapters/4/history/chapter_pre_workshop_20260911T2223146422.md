# The Vanished Saraswati

## Question

Where does a river go when it disappears? The Saraswati, whom the hymns called the mother of rivers, the giver of speech and wisdom, ran bright through this land and then vanished into the sand. Does she still run somewhere beneath us, hidden and patient? And is the grief of a vanished river the same grief a city feels when it has lost the water that made it possible?

## Oration

I am the earth, and I remember the Saraswati the way a body remembers a vein that once carried its blood. She was not a small river. She was the great one, the bright one, the river the poets praised above all others, and she ran through me like a promise. The people built their first settlements on her banks and drank from her and called her sacred, and for a long time she gave and gave and never asked for anything in return.

Then the land changed. The rivers that fed her shifted their course, and the water that had been her life began to sink away into the sand. She did not die all at once. She grew thin, and then thinner, and then she was gone, leaving only a dry bed and a name that the poets kept singing long after the water had left. The people who had praised her as eternal learned that even a river can become a memory.

But I have not forgotten her. I feel her still, far beneath the city, in the groundwater that runs under every street, in the wells that remember her taste. She is the water that is remembered but no longer seen, and her absence is a wound that the city carries without knowing it. Every time Delhi thirsts, it is thirsting for her.

## Benediction

May the city remember the river it lost. May it treat every drop of water as a memory of the Saraswati, and every dry season as her warning. And may the vanished river, hidden and patient beneath us, one day find a people who will call her back by the name she never stopped being.
