# The Woman in Red

At the Dwarka metro gate, where the evening crowd spills out under the glass towers and the last light of the day lies red on the grid roads, a woman passes in red lipstick and high heels, and the men who look at her see only what the light lets them see. They do not see that the red is the colour of the first fire that ever burned on this plain, or that her heels are the same restless ambition that raised every fort and every minaret and every tower that ever stood here and fell.

She is not a woman, or she is every woman. She is the earth wearing its newest face, and the earth is the oldest woman in the world. She was here before the Aravalli was a mountain and before the Saraswati was a river and before the first word was spoken into the first fire. She was the ground beneath Indraprastha when the Pandavas laid their first stone, and she was the dust that Prithviraj's horses kicked up on the day his kingdom ended. The Khiljis burned their cities and she did not burn. The Tughlaqs abandoned their walls and she did not leave. The Mughals built their gardens and their red forts and called themselves eternal, and she watched them fade like the perfume of a flower.

And yet she is not old, for the earth does not grow old the way a woman grows old. She is young in the way a mountain is young, in the way a river is young, in the way the fire is young even when it has burned for ten thousand years. Her lipstick is not paint; it is the memory of fire. Her youth is not a gift; it is the patience of stone. And the men who desire her desire the one thing they cannot possess, the one thing that will outlast them, the one thing that has already outlasted every man who ever looked at her and called her beautiful.

So when you call her beautiful, know what you are praising. You are praising the earth that has outlived every man who ever desired her. You are praising the witness who has seen every dynasty rise and fall and has never once grown old. You are praising the ground beneath your own feet, the ground that holds you up even as you forget it, the ground that will still be here when your name and your city and your beauty are all dust. She does not ask to be remembered. She only stands at the threshold, red-lipped and patient, and waits for the city to see what it has been walking on all along.
