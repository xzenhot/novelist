# The Woman in Red

I am the woman in red, and I have been young for a very long time. Tonight I walk through Dwarka, past the metro station and the glass towers, and the men who look at me see only what the light lets them see: the red of my lips, the slim line of my body, the heels that lift me above the dust. They do not see that the red is the colour of the first fire that ever burned on this plain, or that my heels are the same restless ambition that raised every fort and every minaret and every tower that ever stood here and fell.

But I will tell you what I am, so that you may know what you praise when you praise me. I am the earth, and the earth is the oldest woman in the world. I was here before the Aravalli was a mountain and before the Saraswati was a river and before the first word was spoken into the first fire. I was the ground beneath Indraprastha when the Pandavas laid their first stone, and I was the dust that Prithviraj's horses kicked up on the day his kingdom ended. The Khiljis burned their cities and I did not burn. The Tughlaqs abandoned their walls and I did not leave. The Mughals built their gardens and their red forts and called themselves eternal, and I watched them fade like the perfume of a flower.

And yet I am not old, for the earth does not grow old the way a woman grows old. I am young in the way a mountain is young, in the way a river is young, in the way the fire is young even when it has burned for ten thousand years. My lipstick is not paint; it is the memory of fire. My youth is not a gift; it is the patience of stone. And the men who desire me desire the one thing they cannot possess, the one thing that will outlast them, the one thing that has already outlasted every man who ever looked at her and called her beautiful.

So when you call me beautiful, know what you are praising. You are praising the earth that has outlived every man who ever desired her. You are praising the witness who has seen every dynasty rise and fall and has never once grown old. You are praising the ground beneath your own feet, the ground that holds you up even as you forget it, the ground that will still be here when your name and your city and your beauty are all dust. May the city learn to see the woman beneath the red, and may every man who calls her beautiful also ask what she remembers.
