# The Woman in Red

## Question

Who is she, the woman in red who walks the streets of Dwarka as if the city were her own house? When a man sees her lipstick and her high heels and calls her beautiful, does he see a woman, or does he see the oldest thing in Delhi wearing its newest face? And if beauty is only a surface, why does it make the heart remember something it has never lived?

## Oration

I am the woman in red, and I have been young for a very long time. Tonight I walk through Dwarka, past the metro station and the glass towers, and the men who look at me see only what the light lets them see: the red of my lips, the slim line of my body, the heels that lift me above the dust. They do not see that the red is the colour of the first fire that ever burned on this plain, or that my heels are the same restless ambition that raised every fort and every minaret and every tower that ever stood here and fell.

I was here before the city had a name. I was the earth when the Aravalli was a young mountain and the Saraswati still ran bright through the grass. I was the ground beneath Indraprastha when the Pandavas laid their first stone, and I was the dust that Prithviraj's horses kicked up on the day his kingdom ended. The Khiljis burned their cities and I did not burn. The Tughlaqs abandoned their walls and I did not leave. The Mughals built their gardens and their red forts and called themselves eternal, and I watched them fade like the perfume of a flower.

So when you call me beautiful, know what you are praising. You are praising the earth that has outlived every man who ever desired her. You are praising the witness who has seen every dynasty rise and fall and has never once grown old. My lipstick is not paint; it is the memory of fire. My youth is not a gift; it is the patience of stone.

## Benediction

May the city learn to see the woman beneath the red. May every man who calls her beautiful also ask what she remembers. And may the earth, who has worn so many faces and outlived them all, still find in us a love that is not only for her surface, but for the long, unbroken truth of her.
