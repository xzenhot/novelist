# The Gondwana Drift

The suitcase would not close. Sagorika knelt beside it in the Dwarka room while her beloved pressed the lid, and between their hands a sleeve protruded like a narrow country refusing a border. He was leaving before dawn. For weeks they had spoken of distance as though it were a number that could be folded and put away; now a single garment defeated them. She drew it free and laid it across her knees. Its warmth had already begun to leave.

On his desk lay a drawing titled Gondwana, copied in the loose assurance of a dream. She studied the shapes without pretending to read the earth's exact journey from them. In her imagination, land broke its embraces slowly; a coast became the memory of another coast. Separation had no witness standing safely outside it. Even the witness travelled. She, who had called herself the enduring ground, felt the room move while the suitcase stayed between her feet.

He said that nothing need change. The kindness hurt more than a harsher truth. Her lipstick marked the cup from which they had shared the last tea, and she turned the mark away, ashamed of her desire to preserve so small a shore. Had not kings also begged their stones to keep a moment whole? Their names had gone through her like seasons. Yet this man looking for his ticket was no less beloved because he possessed no kingdom, no less capable of leaving an emptiness.

She gave him the sleeve. Together they folded the garment properly, making space by attention rather than force. The lid descended. That small success released her tears, which had waited through all the large declarations. He sat beside her on the floor. Neither tried to give departure the dignity of a prophecy. Through the window, the first train carried its lit rooms above the sleeping streets; movement had begun everywhere without waiting for their consent.

At the doorway he asked what he should bring when he returned. A true account, she answered. Tell me what altered thee. The request frightened him; she saw that he had hoped to return unchanged to an unchanged woman. But the city within her had no sealed chamber where love could be kept from weather. The river might withdraw, the ridge endure, the season turn; fidelity must find a way to speak across those differences or become another wall around absence.

After he left, she carried the drawing to the balcony. Dawn made its blank spaces luminous. She did not tear the continents apart or fit them back together. She wrote the date of his departure in a corner, a human date, small enough to own honestly. Then she watered the plant he had nearly forgotten. Water sank into the soil, leaving no boundary she could follow.

When his first message came, she read it sitting beside the open suitcase's former place. The room had gained an unfamiliar breadth. She answered from within it, allowing the new distance its name, and leaving room for a voice to cross.
