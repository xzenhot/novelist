# The Tughlaqs

At Tughlaqabad, where the massive walls of the Tughlaq capital still stand empty in the dust and the wind moves through the halls where their voices had been, the city that was abandoned still asks its question. The Tughlaqs dreamed too large and built too fast. They raised a capital of their own, a city of walls and gates and palaces, and they filled it with their ambition and their armies and their plans. And then, for reasons that were part wisdom and part folly, they left it, and the city they had built began to empty, and the wind began to move through the halls where their voices had been.

And the earth held their empty city the way it holds all ruins, without judgment. For the earth does not mourn an abandoned wall; it simply waits, and slowly, patiently, it begins to take the city back. The dust settles in the courtyards, the grass grows through the stones, the birds nest in the arches, and the city that was built to last forever becomes a place where only the wind and the memory of kings still walk. And in this the earth teaches the woven whole, the truth that nothing is separate, that the city and the dust and the grass and the bird are all one thing, and that what the builders called ruin is only the earth reclaiming what was always its own.

There is a truth in the empty city that the full city never learns: that everything we build is borrowed from the earth, and the earth will take it back. The Tughlaqs are gone, and their capital is a ruin, and the ruin is more honest than the city ever was, because it no longer pretends to be forever. It stands there, empty and patient, and it asks the question that every full city refuses to hear: what will you leave behind, and will it be worth the earth's taking back? The walls crumble, and the earth takes them back, and the earth waits.
