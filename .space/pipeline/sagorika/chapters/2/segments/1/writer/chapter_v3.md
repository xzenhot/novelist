# The Aravalli Remembers

On the Delhi Ridge, where the morning walkers step over quartzite and schist and do not know they are stepping on the oldest bone of the earth, the Aravalli lies low and broken and still breathing. It was old before the first word was spoken. It rose when the earth was still learning to be earth, a spine of stone that lifted toward a sky that had no name. The Himalayas came later, young and proud and covered in snow, and the Aravalli watched them rise the way an old woman watches a child grow tall, without envy, because it had already seen everything there was to see.

And it has seen it all, for it is the one blood of this land, the single body from which every living thing here has drawn its life. The forest grew on its slopes and the animals walked its ridges and the rivers ran down its sides, and all of them were limbs of it, and it was the body that held them together. When the first people came, they came for shelter and for stone, and they built their first fires in its shadow, and it held them too, the way a body holds its own children, without asking what they would become.

Then they forgot it. They called it the ridge, and they forgot that it was once a mountain. They cut roads through it and laid their cities over it and buried its slopes under concrete, and still it held them, the way the ground holds everything, without complaint and without end. It remembers the Saraswati when she ran beside it, bright and full, before she vanished into the sand. It remembers the first kings who climbed its slopes to look out over the plain and choose the place where their city would rise. It remembers every dynasty that thought it would last forever, and it remembers how each one of them became dust that settled back into it.

And still it is here, the oldest witness in Delhi, and it has never once looked away. The city walks over it and does not know it. The city cuts it and does not feel it. But it feels everything, and it remembers everything, and it is patient, for it has learned that the only thing that endures is the ground itself, and the ground is it. It does not ask to be remembered. It only waits, half-dead and half-alive, for the city to notice the stone that has been holding it up all along.
