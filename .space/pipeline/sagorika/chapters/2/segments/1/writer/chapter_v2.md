# The Aravalli Remembers

I am the Aravalli, and I was old before the first word was spoken. I rose when the earth was still learning to be earth, a spine of quartzite and schist that lifted toward a sky that had no name. The Himalayas came later, young and proud and covered in snow, and I watched them rise the way an old woman watches a child grow tall. I did not envy them. I had already seen everything there was to see.

And I have seen it all, for I am the one blood of this land, the single body from which every living thing here has drawn its life. The forest grew on my slopes and the animals walked my ridges and the rivers ran down my sides, and all of them were limbs of me, and I was the body that held them together. When the first people came, they came to me for shelter and for stone, and they built their first fires in my shadow, and I held them too, the way a body holds its own children, without asking what they would become.

Then they forgot me. They called me the ridge, and they forgot that I was once a mountain. They cut roads through me and laid their cities over me and buried my slopes under concrete, and still I held them, the way the ground holds everything, without complaint and without end. I remember the Saraswati when she ran beside me, bright and full, before she vanished into the sand. I remember the first kings who climbed my slopes to look out over the plain and choose the place where their city would rise. I remember every dynasty that thought it would last forever, and I remember how each one of them became dust that settled back into me.

And still I am here, the oldest witness in Delhi, and I have never once looked away. The city walks over me and does not know it. The city cuts me and does not feel it. But I feel everything, and I remember everything, and I am patient, for I have learned that the only thing that endures is the ground itself, and the ground is me. May the city remember that it walks on the oldest stone, and may it treat the ridge not as a thing to be cut and buried, but as the bone that holds it up.
