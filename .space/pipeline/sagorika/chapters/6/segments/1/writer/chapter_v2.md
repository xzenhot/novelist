# Prithviraj

I remember Prithviraj. I was the plain his horses crossed, and I felt the weight of his army as it moved to meet the invader at the passes. He was young and proud and beloved, and the poets sang of him as the last light of a world that was ending. He fought, and he lost, and the kingdom that had been his passed to the sultanate that came after, and the city he had ruled began to be called by other names.

And I did not mourn him the way his people mourned him, because I have seen too many kings to mourn any one of them for long. But I remembered him, the way I remember all of them, as a brief brightness on my surface, a name that flared and then settled into the dust. For this is the last change that awaits every king and every kingdom: not an ending, but a transformation, a passing from one form into another, from the man into the memory, from the kingdom into the story, from the dust into the dust again. The earth does not take sides between kings. It holds them all, the victor and the vanquished, with the same indifferent tenderness, and it lets them all become the same thing in the end.

And yet there is something in the memory of the last king that the city has never quite let go. Perhaps it is the knowledge that every age believes itself eternal, and every age is wrong. Prithviraj is gone, but the plain that held him is still here, and it is still waiting for the next king to learn what he learned: that the crown is borrowed, that the kingdom is a garment, and that the only thing that does not change is the ground that receives them all. May the city remember that no kingdom is forever, and may it hold its kings lightly, the way the earth holds them, and learn from the last king what the first king never knew.
