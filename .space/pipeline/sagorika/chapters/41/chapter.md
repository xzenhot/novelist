# The Tethys Sea

At midnight in Dwarka, a glass overturned upon Sagorika's table, and the water travelled towards the little map her beloved had brought. He caught the map by one corner. Too late: a blue country spread beneath his thumb, dissolving the firm edge he had meant to show her. Tethys, he said, reading a word from another page. She heard it as a name spoken through sleep. Outside, the apartments stood in rows of light, each window assuring the darkness that the ground beneath it would remain.

She laid a cloth against the spreading water. She would make no chronicle from a spill, claim no shore beneath this particular floor. Yet imagination opened its unmeasured distance. In that inward sea the towers lost their foundations; a salt wind passed through doors whose owners had believed themselves secure. The woman who was Delhi stood barefoot at an imagined margin, her red mouth the one bright thing the deep had not extinguished. She watched the water approach without granting it a date.

Her beloved laughed at the ruined paper, then ceased. The map had been intended as a gift: here was their future, a room elsewhere, a road leading out, a small square he had circled for possession. Now its streets met in a single stain. Sagorika saw how gently a plan could perish, how little violence water required to change the name of certainty. She remembered the vanished river as thirst remembers a cup, though the river and this dream of ocean were different absences.

He asked whether she wished to leave. Beneath the question she heard the older demand of builders: promise me that thou wilt remain where I have placed thee. She could give him companionship, a shoulder beside his shoulder through a difficult season. She could not give him an earth that had finished becoming. The ridge in her rose worn and stubborn, neither an answer nor a refusal. Its patience was not stillness. Even the oldest bone belonged to a body whose full life no room could contain.

They lifted the wet paper together and hung it above the sink. Drops fell at uneven intervals. Between them the kitchen grew audible: a pipe, a motor, the breathing of two people who had almost mistaken an address for a vow. He erased the square with his finger; its ink travelled into the damp. Then he brought another glass, filled only halfway, and set it where neither elbow would strike it.

By morning the map had dried into ridges. Roads crossed little mountains raised by an ordinary accident. Sagorika folded it carefully, refusing to smooth every wrinkle away. They went out carrying their unfinished destination. Under the lift's bright numbers, her beloved took her hand without asking the ground to promise anything. In the apartment, one pale ring remained upon the table, a shore made by departure, neither wholly lost nor wholly understood by them, awaiting the cloth she would use when she returned.
