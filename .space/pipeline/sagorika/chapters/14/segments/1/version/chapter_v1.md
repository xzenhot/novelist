# The High Heels

Halfway across the paved courtyard, the strap gave way. I had been hurrying towards the lights of Dwarka, measuring the distance by what I might lose if I arrived late. My high heels struck their quick proud rhythm against the ground, then one foot slid sideways and the rhythm broke. I caught the wall with my palm. For a moment shame hurt more sharply than the twisted movement: someone had seen the woman who appeared so certain discover that she could not take another ordinary step.

I sat upon a low edge and examined the loosened strap. It was a small failure, unworthy of the grand names I was tempted to give it. Yet the city had entered that temptation. Every tower seemed to urge me upright; every passing footfall announced that others were continuing while I remained still. The epic called my heels the restlessness of ambition, the wish to rise above dust. Here was the dust itself settling patiently upon my exposed foot, accepting the weight my elegance had tried to distribute elsewhere.

A woman passing with a folded bag asked whether I needed help. I nearly said no. Independence had become another height from which I feared descending. Then I asked if she could hold the bag while I unfastened the other shoe. She waited without curiosity, granting me the privacy that assistance sometimes destroys by making a spectacle of need. When I had both shoes in my hand, she returned the bag and pointed towards a shaded place where I could decide what to do next. Her own errand quietly resumed; mine had changed its hurried pace.

I called the person waiting for me and said I would be late. The words sounded less disastrous once spoken. There would be an inconvenience, perhaps disappointment, but neither required me to punish the foot that had brought me this far. I watched the light move along the courtyard wall. How much of a city is lived in the intervals its ambition calls wasted: the breath recovered, the strap loosened, the hand accepted, the minute in which a person ceases to treat her body as an unwilling servant. Beneath my feet the old ground offered no praise for speed.

I found a way home slowly, carrying the bright shoes by their narrow straps. Their shape still pleased me. I did not have to despise what had failed in order to choose more carefully next time. The city rose around me without requiring that I imitate its height. May our upward longing learn the courtesy of a pause; may the structures we inhabit make room for the bodies that cannot keep their appointed rhythm. At my door I set the shoes down gently. The silence after their clicking was neither defeat nor emptiness. It was the sound in which I could finally hear my own steps, uneven and living, arriving where the evening had left a little space for rest.
