# Ashok — Workshop Metadata

## Workshop Frame

The novel is presented as a memory-narrative: an aging Ashok, decades after Kalinga, dictates edicts and memories to a scribe on a Deccan hill. The modern frame characters are the scribe, the guard, and the hill itself as silent witness. Between chapters, they discuss what they have heard, and the reader watches the past being carved into stone.

## Recurring Workshop Roles

- **The Scribe:** A young literate clerk, skeptical at first, increasingly moved by the emperor's confessions. Asks questions that draw Ashok out.
- **The Guard:** A veteran soldier, loyal, uneasy with the emperor's gentleness, represents the old Mauryan hardness.
- **The Hill / Stone:** Silent witness; the edict stone becomes a character that absorbs the story.

## Narrated-Story Figures

Ashok, Devi, Tishyaraksha, Upagupta, Radhagupta, Susima, and the dead of Kalinga.

## Schedule / Sequence

Tell the story in strict order: Introduction → 1 → 2 → 3 → 4 → 5 → Conclusion. Each chapter's Discussion should lead naturally into the next chapter's Workshop opening.

## Grounding Notes

- The Mauryan empire, 268–232 BCE.
- Pataliputra, Ujjain, Taxila, Kalinga (modern Odisha), Vidisha, the Deccan.
- Religious ferment: Brahmanical ritual, Jain asceticism, Ajivika fatalism, Buddhist sangha.
- Ashok's own rock and pillar edicts are the public evidence of his remorse and dharma policy.
