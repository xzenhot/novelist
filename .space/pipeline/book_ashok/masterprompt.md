# Ashok — Master Prompt

## Book Identity

- **Book name:** ashok
- **Long title:** Ashok: A tormented prince-turned-emperor seeks redemption after a brutal conquest, turning from the sword to the dharma and reshaping an empire through compassion.
- **Form:** novel
- **Language:** en
- **Genre:** historical fiction
- **Era:** ancient India, 3rd century BCE
- **Epic source:** `.space/backlog/epic/ashok/epic.md`

## Style Mandate

Render the novel in a serious, descriptive, image-rich literary register. The chosen signature is **gibran** (Kahlil Gibran, *The Prophet*): biblical, aphoristic, oracular prose-poetry; long flowing sentences; nature as scripture; direct address to the reader; paradox; circular benedictions. The reference is **aurilus** (*The Meditations* of Marcus Aurelius): Stoic introspection, duty, transience, the inner citadel, and the brotherhood of mankind. The theme set is **generic** (the ten Stoic themes). The syntax template is **gibran**.

## Chapter Structure

Each chapter follows the novel's three-section frame:

1. **Workshop** — the modern frame scene where characters discuss the story.
2. **Story** — the narrated historical main material, grounded in the epic.
3. **Discussion** — the characters' response after hearing the story.

The Story section must reach at least 5,500 words unless the pipeline overrides.

## Central Conflict

A man of violent habits tries to become gentle while ruling an empire still trembling from his wars. The novel asks whether an empire founded in blood can be re-founded in regret—and whether regret, repeated often enough, becomes a kind of love.

## Canonical Chapter Order

Introduction → 1 → 2 → 3 → 4 → 5 → Conclusion

The epic `.space/backlog/epic/ashok/epic.md` is the single source of truth for story content. Do not invent story material beyond it.
