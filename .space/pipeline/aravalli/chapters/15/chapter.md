# The Aquifer Below

## Question

What holds the water the city drinks? Beneath the ridge's quartzite lies the aquifer — a slow, dark inheritance of rain gathered over centuries, released in springs, drawn in wells. The poet asks: can a city keep drinking from an archive it does not know it has, and what happens when the archive runs dry?

## Oration

The poem descends to the water table. Every monsoon, the ridge drinks — rain finding the cracks in quartzite, percolating down through sand and fissure into the slow reservoir below. There the water waits in the dark, older than any dynasty, feeding the springs that fed the stepwells, the streams, the first settlements on the flank.

The meditation measures our debt. The modern city pumps the aquifer faster than the ridge can refill it; towers rise where the recharge zones used to catch the rain. The aquifer is the archive's deepest shelf — it keeps no words, only water, and every bottle drawn is a page turned. The poem asks for the discipline of the stepwell-builders: to draw at the speed of replenishment, to keep the catchments open, to treat the underground inheritance as a trust. The mountain stores what the future will need; the present is spending it.

## Benediction

May the hidden water be treated as the visible archive's twin — kept, not merely taken. May the recharge zones be guarded as sacred ground, for they are. And may the city learn to drink at the ridge's pace, so that the aquifer outlasts the thirst that drains it.
