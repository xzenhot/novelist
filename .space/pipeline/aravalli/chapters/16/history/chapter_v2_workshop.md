# The Monsoon Stream

## Question

Where does the rain go? In the monsoon months, the ridge's channels wake — streams that run for a few weeks and vanish, feeding the aquifer and the Yamuna on their way. The poet follows one such stream from its first pool to its disappearance, and asks: what can a waterway that barely lasts a season teach a city built to last forever?

## Oration

The poem walks the streambed in the first heavy rain. Dust becomes current; the channel that was a rumor all year becomes a voice. The ridge sheds its long thirst in a bright rush — over quartzite steps, under fig roots, past the stones the stepwell-builders once aligned to catch exactly this water.

The meditation is on transience as a form of faithfulness. The stream keeps its appointment every year without a contract; it does the ridge's most urgent work — recharging the aquifer, feeding the plains — and then it is gone, leaving wet stone and a smell of wet earth. The city, which wants its water invisible and its rivers buried, has paved many such channels into drains. The poem asks that a few be left open to the sky: a seasonal stream is a ritual the mountain performs, and a city that lets the ritual die has stopped listening to its oldest calendar.

## Benediction

May the seasonal waters be allowed their brief loud lives. May the open channels stay open, and the buried ones be remembered. And may the monsoon find, year after year, at least one stream on the ridge that still runs the old road to the river.
