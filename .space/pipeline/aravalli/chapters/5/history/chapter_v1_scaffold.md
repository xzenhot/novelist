## Oration

A medieval water-harvesting structure that caught the ridge's runoff, linking stone to human ingenuity.

- The poem descends into a medieval stepwell, a water-harvesting structure that caught the ridge's runoff, so that stone and human ingenuity are shown as one continuous act of care.
- The stepwell is treated as a meditation on descent: how a people dug down to meet the water the mountain gave, and how that descent was itself a form of reverence.
- The water of the stepwell is linked to the aquifer of the ridge, reminding the poet that the mountain's gift is not a symbol but a real, life-giving flow.
- The poem closes with the stepwell holding its water in stillness, a figure for the disciplined heart that receives without hoarding, and the reader is left with the quiet depth of what the stone has gathered.
