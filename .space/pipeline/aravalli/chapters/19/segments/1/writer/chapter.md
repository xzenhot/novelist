# The Forest Hermitage

## Question

What does a mountain teach those who sit still on it? In the ridge's forest, hermits made their cells — root and stone, silence and chant — and the mountain became their teacher without pronouncing a word. I sit where a stone bench may once have been, and I ask: what did the still ones learn from the moving rock, and can any of it be written down?

## Oration

The hermitage keeps its own hours, and the poem follows them. Dawn: mist in the trees, the chant low and unhurried, breath and birdsong alternating like two halves of one sentence. Noon: heat pooled in the shade, the chant gone inward, the forest silent in the particular way of dry deciduous forest at its stillest. Evening: the chant slows as the light goes, and the birds take over the service, and the difference between worship and listening narrows to nothing.

Why did the still ones choose this ridge, of all places — this dry, scrubby, unfertile spine? Precisely for its patience. A mountain is the only teacher that has outlasted every lesson it gives. The hermits did not quarry it, did not cut it, did not even build on it beyond a bench and a fire-ring; they sat down inside its endurance the way other people sit down inside a book. Their discipline — sitting with what is, until what is becomes legible — is the discipline the ridge itself performs. Geology is patience spiritualized: pressure accepted without protest, time endured without haste, form revealed only when it is ready. The fold took ages to become visible. The hermits were content to take ages too.

They were the ridge's readers before the geologists — reading by a different method, arriving at a different but related finding: that what endures is what is true, and what is true needs no hurry. And the mountain, for its part, answered them the only way it answers anyone: by continuing. By being, every morning, exactly what it was the evening before. For a person practicing stillness, there is no better company.

The forest kept their cells secret long after the chanting stopped — hid the bench, absorbed the fire-ring, took the paths back. Some of the archive is silence. This sequence, which has insisted on naming and recording, must end its work here by leaving room for the unnamed: the lesson that can be sat with but not written down.

## Benediction

May the still ones' bench remain under the trees — unmarked, but not unkept. May the forest that hid the hermitage keep hiding it, and keep teaching it. And may whoever climbs the ridge in silence receive the one lesson the mountain gives: endure, and become legible.
