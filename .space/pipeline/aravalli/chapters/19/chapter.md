# The Forest Hermitage

## Question

What does a mountain teach those who sit still on it? In the ridge's forest, hermits made their cells — root and stone, silence and chant — and the mountain became their teacher without a word. The poet asks: what did the still ones learn from the moving rock, and can it be written down?

## Oration

The poem enters the hermitage at different hours: dawn mist in the sal trees, noon heat pooled in the shade, evening when the chanting slows and the birds take over. The hermits chose the ridge not despite its barrenness but because of its patience — a mountain is the only teacher that has outlasted every lesson it gives.

The meditation joins the still ones to the stone. The hermit's discipline — sitting with what is, until what is becomes legible — is the discipline the ridge itself performs. Geology is patience spiritualized: pressure accepted, time endured, form revealed without haste. The hermits did not quarry the mountain; they studied it the way water studies stone, and the forest kept their cells secret long after the chanting stopped. The poet, who writes, understands that he is the hermits' debtor: their stillness taught the mountain's legibility, and every line of this sequence sits on their bench.

## Benediction

May the still ones' bench remain under the trees, unmarked but not unkept. May the forest that hid the hermitage keep hiding it, and keep teaching it. And may whoever climbs the ridge in silence receive the one lesson the mountain gives: endure, and become legible.
