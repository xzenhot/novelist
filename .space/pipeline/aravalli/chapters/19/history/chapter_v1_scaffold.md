## Oration

A hermitage in the ridge's forest becomes a figure for the contemplative life lived close to stone.

- The poem sets a hermitage in the ridge's forest, so that the contemplative life lived close to stone is dramatized, and the reader is shown the quiet of the ashram.
- The hermitage is treated as a meditation on withdrawal, so that the poet reflects on how the forest and the ridge offered a place apart, and how that apartness was itself a form of attention.
- The hermitage is linked to the later city, so that the contrast between the quiet forest and the loud city is drawn, and the reader is asked what has been lost in the movement from one to the other.
- The poem ends with the hermitage empty, so that the reader learns that the contemplative life has withdrawn from the ridge, but that its silence remains in the stone.
