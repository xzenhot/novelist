# The Hand-Axe Maker

## Question

Who was he, the man at the core? Among the many hunters who worked the ridge, the poem imagines one — the maker whose edge was so true that we hold it now. What did he see that others did not, and what did his excellence cost him, and give him?

## Oration

The poem narrows to a single figure: a maker at the outcrop, apart from the band, turning a core of quartzite in his hands until he finds the angle inside it. He strikes, examines, strikes again. The flakes fall like small decisions. Where the others work quickly, he works until the edge runs true — and the poem reads in that patience the first signature in the archive: not a name, but a standard.

The meditation honors craft as the beginning of culture. Before writing, before worship, someone cared about the quality of an edge beyond its use — and that caring is the seed of everything after: the altar's fit, the well's symmetry, the wall's coursing, the poem's line. The maker never knows his tool will lie in a museum case under glass. He only knows the stone has a right shape in it, and that finding it is his work. The poet, choosing his words with the same stubborn attention, recognizes a colleague across a hundred millennia and bows.

## Benediction

May every hand that shapes anything care as the maker cared. May the standard he set — the true edge, the patient strike — remain the measure of all work done on this ridge. And may the poem, like the axe, be finished only when nothing false remains in it.
