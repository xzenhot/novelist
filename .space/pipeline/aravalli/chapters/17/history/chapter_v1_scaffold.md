## Oration

The anonymous prehistoric maker of the hand-axe becomes a figure for the first human artist.

- The poem imagines the anonymous prehistoric maker of the hand-axe, so that the first human artist is given a face, and the reader is shown the patience of the first craft.
- The maker is treated as a meditation on origin, so that the poet reflects on how every later maker — the mason, the sculptor, the builder — descends from this first hand that shaped stone.
- The hand-axe is linked to the poem itself, so that the act of making a tool and the act of making a poem are shown to be one continuous human gesture.
- The poem ends with the maker's hand resting, so that the reader learns that the first artist is not lost but present in every later act of making, and that the ridge holds that first hand's work.
