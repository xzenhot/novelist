# The Public Hearing

## Question

Can a mountain have a hearing? In a room of laminated tables and numbered agendas, a planning body takes up the fate of the last unprotected ridge land. Surveys are read; objections are filed; objections are answered. Seated among the citizens, I ask the only question that matters in such a room: what would it mean for the ridge to be represented here — not as acreage, but as the oldest continuous witness the city has?

## Oration

Follow the procedure, because procedure is where the mountain's fate is actually decided. The developer's projection goes up: towers rendered at golden hour, land values rising like the sun in the picture. The hydrologist answers with a map — the aquifer's slow catchment drawn under the very footprints in the render, recharge zones shaded in careful blue. The historian contributes one page on the quarry era. Then a retired teacher stands, unfolds an old survey, and reads the mentor's findings into the record, line by line, her voice the only sound in the room that has no budget attached to it.

The turn of the poem is quiet. One by one the exhibits are gathered — the hand-axe, the altar stone, the stepwell, the wall, the quarry scar — and entered not as separate items but as a single exhibit: one continuous record, too layered to be replaced, too complete to be called vacant. The straight-line case for development meets the curved-line case of the fold, and for once the curved line holds.

Then the reprieve: boundaries drawn, land notified, the file closed with the ridge inside the protection rather than outside it. And the poem refuses, absolutely, to celebrate. A reprieve is not a resurrection; the lost layer stays lost, and the notification is only as durable as the memory that sustains it — as durable as the next election, the next amendment, the next room of tired officials. The mountain was not present in this room. It was spoken for. The teacher spoke; the map spoke; the exhibits spoke; and the arrangement now is permanent: the ridge's voice is a trust, held by whoever has learned to read it, and renounceable by anyone who stops.

The flame of the case, once entered in the record, does not go out by itself. It is kept or it is not kept. That is the whole law.

## Benediction

May every hearing keep the record open and the evidence whole. May those who speak for the mountain speak accurately — without decoration, in the ridge's own sober register. And may the reprieve become a habit of the city, renewed hearing by hearing, so that the mountain is never again decided about in its absence.
