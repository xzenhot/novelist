## Oration

The argument made explicit before a planning body, and the ridge's reprieve.

- The poem sets its scene at a public hearing, where the argument for the ridge is made explicit before a planning body, so that the book's central question is spoken aloud rather than only felt.
- The poet reflects on how the mountain's case must be made in the language of the city — evidence, maps, records — so that the ancient stone is translated into the terms the modern world can hear.
- The hearing is treated as a threshold, a place where the ridge's fate is weighed, and the poem honors the discipline of making the case without sentiment.
- The poem ends with the decision deferred and the ridge granted a reprieve, so that the reader learns that witness can buy time, and that a reprieve is not a victory but a chance to remember.
