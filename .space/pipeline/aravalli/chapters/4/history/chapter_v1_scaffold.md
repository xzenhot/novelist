## Oration

A hermitage in the ridge's forest, a fire-altar of ridge stone, and the first hymns to the rivers the ridge feeds.

- The poem sets a Vedic hermitage in the ridge's forest, where a fire-altar is built of ridge stone, and the first hymns name the rivers the ridge feeds, so that the sacred and the geological meet.
- Fire becomes a figure for the human and the divine, and the poet searches for the ridge's water inside the hymn, as if grace were hidden in the very stone of the altar.
- The contrast between the ancient forest and the modern city is drawn without nostalgia, so that the sacred is not mourned as lost but sought as continuous.
- The poem ends by suggesting that the fire's light is not elsewhere but within, a radiance that can be found in the stone if the eye is patient enough to look, carrying the reader toward the stepwell.
