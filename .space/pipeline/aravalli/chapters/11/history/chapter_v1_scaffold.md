## Oration

The oral memory of the ridge — the names, the seasons, the people — entrusted to be recorded.

- The poem gives voice to the ridge elder, who tells the oral memory of the ridge — the names, the seasons, the people — so that the archive is shown to live not only in stone but in memory.
- The elder's story is treated as a sacred trust, so that the poem honors the living link between the geological and the human archive, and the reader is taught to listen.
- The names and seasons are named concretely, so that the oral record is grounded in the particular, and the poem refuses to let it dissolve into abstraction.
- The poem ends with the elder entrusting his knowledge to be recorded, so that the reader learns that memory is a gift passed from hand to hand, and that to receive it is to become its keeper.
