## Oration

A survey of surviving ridge fragments becomes a map of what memory must protect.

- The poem lets a survey of surviving ridge fragments summon the memory of a mentor who spent his life warning that the ridge was being quarried to death, so that the map becomes a map of what memory must protect.
- The map is established with care: the poet names each surviving outcrop but immediately qualifies the act, insisting that a map is not the mountain, only a promise to remember it.
- Stone imagery — quartzite, granite, fold, outcrop — begins to gather as the poem's recurring vocabulary, binding the geological and the human into a single current.
- The poem ends by holding both worlds in balance, the map and the mountain, so that the reader understands the sequence's central discipline: to document without possessing, to remember without replacing.
