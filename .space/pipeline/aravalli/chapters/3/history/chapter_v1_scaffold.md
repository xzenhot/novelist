## Oration

A band of hunters knaps quartzite on the ridge, leaving tools for the future to find.

- The poem dramatizes a band of prehistoric hunters knapping quartzite on the ridge, so that the first human layer of the archive is felt as a living scene rather than a museum label.
- The act of knapping is treated as a meditation on making: how a hand and a stone produce a tool, and how that tool, abandoned, becomes a message to the future.
- The quartzite of the ridge is linked to the earth and to mortality, reminding the poet that the first makers were bodies like ours, and that their work is not his to claim but to receive.
- The poem closes with the hunters' camp abandoned and the tools left behind, a figure for the disciplined heart that makes and then lets go, and the reader is left with the quiet pressure of what the stone remembers.
