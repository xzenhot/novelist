# The Prehistoric Hunters

## Question

Who left these tools, and what did they hope the future would find? Long before Delhi had a name, hunters knapped quartzite on this ridge, beside a stream that has since gone underground. I stand where they knelt, on flake-scatter the rain has slowly uncovered, and I ask the question that has no address to travel to: when the maker struck his last deliberate flake, was he thinking of the animal, the fire, the evening — or, faintly, of us?

## Oration

Dawn on the outcrop, in the time before names. Smoke stands up straight from a temporary camp; the air smells of wet ash and crushed grass. The band works the edge of the quartzite exposure, where the stone breaks the way they like it — clean, conchoidal, predictable to a hand that has learned it. The hammer-stone rises and falls. Flakes drop with a sound like small decisions being made. A child watches from the fold of a rock, learning the angle without being taught it.

They read the ridge the way their descendants would read scripture — for guidance in living. Where the quartzite breaks true. Where the game crosses between water and grazing ground. Where the stream gathers after rain, running east toward the great river's shifting channels. The mountain teaches, and they answer with skill: cores trimmed, platforms prepared, edges finished by the patient removal of flakes no bigger than fingernails. It is not mere utility. A hand-axe carries a mental template, an idea of *right* that existed before the tool and outlasted it — the first standard, the first art that admits it is not art.

And they left no names. That is the fact I keep turning over, the way the maker turned the core. Everything our age builds is signed, branded, attributed, claimed. They signed nothing and claimed nothing, and so they were free to make the thing itself excellent. The name would have faded with their language, which has faded entirely; the edge survives. What lasts is not the name but the made thing — this is the lesson of the flake-scatter, offered without a lesson-giver.

I pick up a flake that missed nothing, a byproduct of an afternoon's work, and feel the conversation continue: stone answering stone, hand answering hand, across a depth of time the mind refuses and then, suddenly, accepts. They could not have intended me. But intention is not the only channel. Skill is a channel; care is a channel; the discipline of doing a thing well is a channel that stays open as long as someone downstream is willing to hold up an end.

The first makers and the latest builders are one continuous people. That is not sentiment; it is continuity, the plainest reading of the evidence: the same ridge, the same hands, one long project of shaping. Every outcrop is a possible page of what they wrote. I have come to read a sentence or two and carry them back to the city that no longer remembers it is descended from knappers.

## Benediction

May the patience of the first makers steady whoever walks this ridge today. May every tool recovered be read as a letter, not a relic — addressed to whoever has hands. And may we prove worthy of what the hunters left: a record that begins in stone, and must not end there.
