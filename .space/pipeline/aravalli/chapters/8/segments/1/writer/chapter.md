# The Continuous Record

## Question

What if all of it is one document? I lay the evidence side by side on the long table of the mind: the hand-axe, the fire-altar, the stepwell, the fort wall, the quarry face. Five objects from five worlds. Are they separate relics, each with its own little story — or pages of a single continuous record, written on one ridge by one unbroken people?

## Oration

Read the ridge as a shelf. On it, in order: a flake struck by a hunter whose language we cannot guess, shaped by a patience older than farming. Beside it, an altar stone from a forest terrace, dark at one corner where a kept fire leaned for years. Beside that, a dressed block from a stepwell's gallery, its mason's line still faintly visible, that held back the monsoon for a city's thirst. Then a squared block of rampart quartzite, raised by men who answered to sultans. And last, a blasted scar of quarry face, powder-burnt, where machines of my own century took the mountain in tons instead of blocks.

Five eras. One material. Every era wrote on the same quartzite, in its own hand, and the ridge never once refused the pen. That is the fact this sequence has been building toward, the quiet revelation underneath the whole sequence: history here is not a series of separate cities that happened to share a postcode. It is a single handwriting that changes slant — hunter to hermit, hermit to well-keeper, well-keeper to mason, mason to contractor, and now to the walker with a notebook who is, in truth, only the latest slant of the same hand.

The record is continuous because the mountain is continuous. The stone outlasted every hand that wrote on it, and precisely by outlasting, it joined them — the way a page joins all the sentences written on it. Pull one thread and the weave trembles: erase the stepwell and the covenant of water loses its witness; erase the altar and the first prayers lose their proof; erase the hand-axe and the whole book loses its opening line.

So I understand, finally, what the obligation is. A record this continuous deserves a reader this faithful. Erasure anywhere breaks the whole document — and keeping it whole is not nostalgia. It is the present's only way of signing itself honestly into the archive.

## Benediction

May the pages of the ridge be kept in their order — tool, altar, well, wall, wound — so that no era reads alone. May the city recognize its own handwriting in the older hands, and tremble, as one thread trembles, at every erasure. And may the continuity that stone preserved be preserved in turn by memory, which is the only pen the mountain has left.
