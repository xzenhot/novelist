# The Lost Layer

## Question

What is gone, and who will say it out loud? The poet walks the quarried outcrops, the buried streams, the sites that exist now only in old surveys, and asks: when a layer of the record is erased, does the mountain grieve — and if it does, who hears it?

## Oration

The poem becomes an inventory of absence. Here, an outcrop mapped a century ago is now a flat cleaned lot; the quartzite that recorded the fold is crushed into road metal. Here, a stream that the stepwell caught runs in a storm drain, unnamed. Here, a site that held the ridge's memory was graded flat before anyone thought to look. Each loss is specific, and the poem insists on the specificity — grief for geology must be as exact as grief for people.

The meditation is ethical, not sentimental. The mountain does not mourn; it endures. But the record loses pages, and a reader who arrives late cannot recover what was never transcribed. The poet counts what remains, and the counting is an act of witness: he names the lost layer so that its absence stays in the record it left. The ridge is diminished but unbroken — and the difference between those two words is the whole discipline of this poem.

## Benediction

May what was lost be named, so that absence keeps a place in the archive. May the quarried faces and buried streams be remembered as accurately as the surviving shrines. And may the city feel the missing pages when it reads its own history, so that no further layer goes unrecorded into the dark.
