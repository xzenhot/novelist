## Oration

The quarried outcrops, the buried streams, the erased sites — what has already been lost.

- The poem confronts what has already been lost — the quarried outcrops, the buried streams, the erased sites — so that the sequence's darkest turn is faced honestly rather than avoided.
- The poet refuses to romanticize the loss, naming the specific places that are gone, so that grief is grounded in the particular rather than the abstract.
- The lost layer is linked to the larger question of memory, so that the poem asks what a civilization becomes when it buries its own evidence.
- The poem ends with the poet standing before the absence, so that the reader learns that true reverence must face what is gone, not turn away from it, and that grief is itself a form of witness.
