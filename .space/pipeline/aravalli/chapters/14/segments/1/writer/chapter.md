# Quartzite And Granite

## Question

Why two stones, and not one? The ridge carries quartzite — metamorphosed, glassy, resistant — and the wider Aravalli craton carries granite and gneiss — coarse, crystalline, slow-risen. I walk the boundary country where the temperaments change underfoot, and I ask: what are the two natures of this mountain, and which one does the city resemble?

## Oration

First stone: quartzite, the ridge's endurance. Sandstone that went down into the earth and was changed — heat and pressure closing its grains into something that rings under the hammer, that sheds rain, that weathers into the long resistant spines a ridge is made of. Its whole character is pressure remembered and outlasted. Strike it and the sound comes back clean, the way a kept promise comes back.

Second stone: granite, the mountain's interior. Not squeezed but cooled — risen slowly from depth, crystallizing without haste, its feldspar and mica large enough to catch light like held breath. Its character is fire remembered and forgiven: once molten, now the most patient solid on earth. Where quartzite remembers what was done to it, granite remembers what it was.

The ridge belongs to the quartzite; the granite belongs to the deeper craton the Aravalli rises from — and the boundary country, where the two temperaments meet in outcrop, is the mountain's true portrait. A body of more than one endurance. It needed both: the resistant skin that holds the line against weather, and the deep crystalline root that remembers the original fire.

And the city? Delhi is quartzite and granite both. The surface is all quartzite — taking whatever pressure history applies, invasion, partition, migration, construction, and keeping its form, ringing clean under the hammer of events. The depth is granite — older populations, older waters, older griefs, cooled slowly into what the city is at its core. Surface that endures pressure; depth that remembers fire. Neither temperament alone would survive; together they are what a city is.

So the poem refuses to choose between the stones, and the reader should refuse too. The record the ridge keeps is written in their joint hand — pressure's memory and fire's memory, two inks, one page. To quarry either without reading both is to tear a page written in two inks at once.

## Benediction

May the two stones keep their conversation beneath our feet. May the city learn endurance from the quartzite and depth from the granite. And may whoever reads the ridge honor both temperaments — pressure's memory and fire's memory — as the two hands that wrote the mountain's book.
