## Oration

The two stones of the ridge — quartzite and granite — become emblems of hardness and endurance.

- The poem distinguishes the two stones of the ridge, quartzite and granite, so that their different hardnesses become emblems of different kinds of endurance.
- Quartzite is treated as the stone of edges, the stone that breaks into tools, while granite is the stone of mass, the stone that resists, and the poet honors both.
- The two stones are linked to the two faces of memory, so that the sharp record of the tool and the massive record of the mountain are shown to be one archive.
- The poem ends with the poet holding both stones, so that the reader learns that endurance has more than one form, and that the ridge is made of both the edge and the mass.
