## Oration

The Vedic hymns that name the rivers the ridge feeds become a figure for the sacred naming of water.

- The poem recalls the Vedic hymns that name the rivers the ridge feeds, so that the sacred naming of water is dramatized, and the reader is shown the first poetry of the place.
- The hymn is treated as a meditation on naming, so that the poet reflects on how to name a river is to honor it, and how the ridge's water was once named and sung.
- The hymn is linked to the modern silence, so that the contrast between the sung rivers and the buried streams is drawn, and the reader is asked what is lost when water is no longer named.
- The poem ends with the hymn fading, so that the reader learns that the sacred naming has grown quiet, but that the poem itself is an attempt to name the water again.
