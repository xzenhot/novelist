# The Fold Of Time

## Question

What shape does time take? The geologists read the ridge's rock and find it bent — strata once laid flat now folded and upended by pressures older than animals. The poet asks: if the stone teaches that even the level ground has been folded, what does that teach us about the past we assume is straight?

## Oration

The poem learns from the fold. What was horizontal became vertical; what was sea-floor became hilltop; what took a billion years to bend is read in an afternoon. Time, the ridge says, is not a line but a fold — eras pressed together until the deepest and the nearest touch, the prehistoric hand-axe lying yards from the metro pile.

The meditation turns on perspective. The city plans in decades and mistakes its plans for permanence; the fold corrects the error without cruelty. At the ridge's scale, the present is a thin and recent layer — but it is the only layer that can still choose what gets written next. The poem ends with the axis of the fold stretching away under the city, the imperial geometry of its straight roads having passed, while the mountain's curved line remains — the shape no straight line could erase, holding all the eras it touches in one long embrace.

## Benediction

May we read the fold and distrust the line. May the deep layers press their patience into the thin present. And may whoever walks the ridge feel the eras touch where the strata bend, and walk more humbly for it.
