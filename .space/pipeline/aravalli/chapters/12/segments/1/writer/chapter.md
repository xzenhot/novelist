# The Mountain Remembers

## Question

What does the mountain remember of us — and will it outlast our forgetting? Night, and the sequence stands still for its closing vision: the ridge surviving, diminished but unbroken, beneath the modern skyline. The city's lights rise against the dark shoulder of quartzite. I ask the oldest question in this book: when every witness is gone, who testifies to the testimony?

## Oration

Stand where the ridge meets the city's glare and hold both in one gaze. Below, the metropolis rewrites itself nightly in glass and traffic — towers climbing, roads widening, the whole fabric in a churn of renewal that erases as it builds. Above, the fold keeps its ancient outline. The silhouette is nearly unchanged — the same long curve the first hunters saw breaking the horizon — though the body beneath it is quarried, reduced, scarred. The outline holds because outline is what a billion years does best.

The mountain remembers — and the poem must be precise about how, because imprecision here curdles into sentiment. It does not remember as a mind remembers. It remembers as structure: every fold a sentence, every seam a clause, every scar a paragraph, written in a script the earth has been perfecting since before there were readers. The human chapters are recent lines in a very old hand — thin as the skin of the present is thin — but they are written on the same body, and the body holds them. The city that forgets is itself recorded on the ridge it forgets. Erasure is written too.

And here the sequence's first movement resolves. The wonder of the oldest stone, the grief of the lost layer, the covenant of the hearing — they settle into one understanding: the archive outlasts the archivists. This is not despair. It is the strangest comfort available to a responsible creature: the record does not need me to survive. It needed me only to be read aloud while I could — to be carried from stone into speech, from speech into other readers, while the carrying was possible.

One body, after all: the skyline drinks from the aquifer the ridge holds; the towers stand on the plain the ridge gathers water for; every erasure is a wound to a body both share. The lights and the dark shoulder are not opposites. They are the same creature, briefly lit at one end.

## Benediction

May the mountain's memory outlast our forgetting, as it has outlasted every empire. May the city sleep each night against a witness it does not disturb. And may the reader of these pages join the long line — of those who held a stone, read a fold, and said: this is real, this is old, and this must remain.
