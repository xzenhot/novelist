# The Mountain Remembers

## Question

What does the mountain remember of us — and will it outlast our forgetting? The sequence closes its first arc with a vision: the ridge surviving, diminished but unbroken, beneath the modern skyline. Lights of the city rise against the dark shoulder of quartzite. The poet asks the oldest question in the book: when every witness is gone, who testifies to the testimony?

## Oration

The poem stands at night where the ridge meets the city's glare. Below, the metropolis rewrites itself in glass and traffic; above, the fold keeps its ancient outline, unchanged in silhouette though quarried in body. The mountain remembers — not with consciousness, but with structure: every fold, every seam, every scar is a sentence in a script the earth has been writing for a billion years, and the human chapters are recent lines in a very old hand.

The vision resolves the sequence's first movement. Wonder at the stone, grief for the lost layer, the covenant of witness — all settle into a single understanding: the archive outlasts the archivists. The city that forgets is itself recorded on the ridge it forgets; erasure is written too. The poet accepts his position honestly — a brief reader of a long document — and finds that this humility is not despair but a strange comfort: the record does not need him to survive. It needs him only to be read aloud while he can.

## Benediction

May the mountain's memory outlast our forgetting, as it has outlasted every empire. May the city sleep each night against a witness it does not disturb. And may the reader of these pages join the long line of those who held a stone, read a fold, and said: this is real, this is old, and this must remain.
