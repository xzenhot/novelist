## Oration

The first fire kindled on the ridge becomes a figure for the beginning of human warmth and ritual.

- The poem imagines the first fire kindled on the ridge, so that the beginning of human warmth and ritual is dramatized, and the reader is shown the first circle of light in the dark.
- The fire is treated as a meditation on beginning, so that the poet reflects on how every later fire — the altar, the hearth, the lamp — descends from this first flame.
- The first fire is linked to the stone that sparked it, so that the ridge is shown to be the source not only of tools but of warmth and light.
- The poem ends with the fire burning low, so that the reader learns that the first fire is not extinguished but carried forward, and that the ridge holds the memory of that first warmth.
