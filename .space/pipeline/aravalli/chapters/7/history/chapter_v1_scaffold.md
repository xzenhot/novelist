## Oration

The honest voice of development, arguing that the city's growth outweighs a ridge of old stone.

- The poem gives voice to the contractor, the honest advocate of development, so that the argument that the city's growth outweighs a ridge of old stone is heard in full rather than dismissed.
- The contractor's case is treated with seriousness, so that the poem refuses the easy comfort of a villain, and the reader is asked to weigh the living city against the ancient stone.
- The poem names the real need — roads, housing, water, work — so that the conflict is not sentiment against greed but two genuine goods in tension.
- The poem ends with the question left open, so that the reader is drawn into the same ethical test, asked to consider what a civilization owes to the mountain that made it possible.
