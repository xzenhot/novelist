## Oration

The poet holds a quartzite hand-axe and recognizes the ridge as the oldest witness in Delhi.

- The poem opens with the poet holding a quartzite hand-axe on a surviving outcrop of the Delhi Ridge, and the encounter is framed as recognition rather than spectacle, a stone that seems already known before it is named.
- The ridge's age is felt against the city's haste, so that the billion-year mountain and the generation-fast city stand side by side, and the sacred is discovered inside the ordinary rather than imported into it.
- The first stirring of the archive metaphor is introduced gently, as a faint resemblance the poet notices but does not yet impose, guarding the stone's reality from the very first line.
- The poem closes by asking whether a mountain can be the oldest witness of a civilization, leaving the question open so that the reader is carried forward into the next poem with a quieter, cleaner gaze.
