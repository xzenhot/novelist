## Oration

The first large-scale extraction of the mountain, when the ridge was cut to build forts.

- The poem turns to the medieval quarry, where the ridge was first cut on a large scale to build forts, so that the mountain's wound is named honestly rather than romanticized.
- The quarry is treated as a turning point: the moment the mountain became a resource to be taken, and the poem refuses to soften that violence even as it honors the forts the stone built.
- The cut stone is linked to the larger question of what a civilization takes and what it leaves, so that the quarry becomes a figure for every extraction that follows.
- The poem ends with the quarry still open, so that the reader learns that the mountain's wound is not healed but ongoing, and that memory must include the taking as well as the gift.
