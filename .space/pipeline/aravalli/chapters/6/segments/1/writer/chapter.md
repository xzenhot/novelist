# The Sultan's Quarry

## Question

When did the first blow fall that was meant to remove the mountain rather than understand it? In the sultanate centuries, the ridge was cut on a new scale — its quartzite squared into walls, gateways, and water channels for the forts of Delhi. I stand at an old quarry face where the chisel marks still grid the stone like writing, and I ask: is a quarry a wound, a harvest, or the moment a civilization begins to spend an inheritance it cannot replace?

## Oration

Listen to what the stone remembers. For millennia the ridge gave up only what the hand could carry — a flake here, a blade there, the mountain unnoticeably diminished. Then the cities came, and the scale changed. Whole faces of quartzite left the mountain and entered history: dressed into rampart courses, laid into gateways, set as steps to halls where the business of empires was done. The stone that had memorized folding and fire now memorized courts.

I will not pretend the beauty is false. The walls are handsome; the coursing is masterly; a fortress built of the ridge defended the city the ridge made possible. The masons were artists of their trade, and their chisel marks, crossing the ancient bedding of the rock, are a kind of conversation — two disciplines of pressure meeting in one block. If the mountain must change garment, it could hardly have been changed into anything worthier than walls that still stand.

But mark the moment exactly, because everything after depends on it. The hunters took flakes; the sultans took faces. For the first time, the archive itself became raw material. Pages were lifted out of the ridge's book and bound into the city's. And the mountain, old enough to endure any labor, endured this too — the way an old body endures work, without complaint, but not without record. The record is the quarry face itself: chisel-grid over fold, the recent handwriting scored across the ancient, a scar with a date written all over it in the only language stone keeps.

Every later extraction begins here. The contractor's machines in the next century of this sequence, the graded lots, the crushed road-metal — all descend from this first agreement, made in the sultanate centuries, that the ridge was a reserve of building material awaiting permission. The mountain remembers being read, and it remembers being cut, and it has never once confused the two.

## Benediction

May the beauty of the walls never be separated from the cost of the stone. May the raw quarry faces be read as margins where the mountain showed its interior — and may the city that rose from the ridge learn, at last, to build without removing the witness that makes its history possible.
