Gibran's *The Prophet* voice (biblical, aphoristic, oracular) is the natural carrier for this meditative material, and the ten Stoic themes in `generic.md` map directly onto the epic's thematic threads.

## Theme Assignments

Each chapter is assigned one Stoic theme (cycled from `generic.md`), mapped onto a present-day concern.

| Chapter | Name | Theme | Contemporary Mapping |
|---------|------|-------|----------------------|
| 1 | The Noise | The Fading Name | The endless feed and the talking glass — the modern cult of visibility, being seen and liked, and the hollow vanity of reputation. |
| 2 | The Turning | The Inner Citadel | The search for refuge in an always-on world — the need for an inner space no notification can reach. |
| 3 | The Realization | The Woven Whole | The modern sense of fragmentation and isolation — the realization that the self is not separate from the whole (*Tat tvam asi*). |
| 4 | The Living | The Royal Service | The modern question of meaning and purpose — finding nobility in presence and service rather than status. |
| 5 | The Echo | The Last Change | The modern fear of death and impermanence — the understanding that the self does not die, for it was never born. |

## Weaving Notes

- **Embody, don't explain** — each theme is carried through image and cadence, not stated directly.
- **Return to the metaphor family** — each chapter draws on its theme's imagery (e.g. "writing in sand" for The Fading Name, "the still lake" for The Inner Citadel).
- **Honor the signature** — Gibran's three-part form (the question, the answer, the benediction) carries each theme.
- **Ground in the epic** — every theme is native to the epic's threads (the noise and the silence, the question, the turning, the realization, the return).
