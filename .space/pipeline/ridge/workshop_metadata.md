# Workshop Metadata — Ridge

## Workshop Frame

Each chapter opens with the narrator arriving at a specific location at a specific hour. The Workshop section establishes:

- The site (one of the five Aravalli fragments)
- The season and time of day
- The sensory threshold: what the narrator sees, hears, smells on arrival
- The human figure who will anchor the chapter (forest guard, birder, ecologist, developer, student)

## Narrated Story Figures (non-human)

- The ridge leopard (chapters 1, 7)
- Migratory birds — bar-headed goose, painted stork, common pochard (chapter 5)
- The dhau tree (chapter 1, Conclusion)
- Sambar deer (chapter 3)
- The monsoon (chapters 2, 9)

## Sequence Notes

Chapters move geographically south to north (Mangar Bani → Jasola Bhatti → Buddh Jayanti → JNU → Okhla), then inward (the corridor, the children, the reckoning). This mirrors the ridge itself: fragmented but directional.

## Grounding Notes

- All species names verified against Salim Ali and Grimmett/Inskipp for birds; Pradip Krishen for trees.
- Historical dates for Aravalli formation: ~1.5 billion years BP (Precambrian).
- Mangar Bani sacred grove: protected by community decree, not gazette — this distinction is the chapter's thesis.
- JNU campus biodiversity: formally surveyed by JNU faculty; data available in departmental reports.
- Okhla Bird Sanctuary: annual counts by BNHS and local birding groups.
